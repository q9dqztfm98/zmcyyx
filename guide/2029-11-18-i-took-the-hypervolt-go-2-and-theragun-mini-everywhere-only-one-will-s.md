# I Took the Hypervolt Go 2 and Theragun Mini Everywhere. Only One Will Stay in My Gym Bag.

[Mini Everywhere Only](https://incretin-path.escortskhi.com/blog/0043703842.html)

PERCUSSION MASSAGERS HAVE become a go-to tool for easing soreness and prepping muscles pre-workout, but hardly anyone wants to shell out $300 or lug around a bulky device. That’s why the two penultimate brands (Hyperice and Therabody) make compact models like the Hypervolt Go 2 and Theragun Mini that have exploded in popularity. These massage guns are lighter, more affordable, and easier to stash in a gym bag without sacrificing much in performance. For those that travel, they offer the opportunity to bring your self-care regime on the road, too.

The Hyperice Hypervolt Go 2 looks how you’d expect a massage gun to look—sleek, pistol-shaped, and built with clean lines that give it a modern edge. It comes with two attachments, weighs just over a pound, and costs $139 (nearly a third of the Hypervolt 2 Pro’s price tag).

This is your standard-issue model, and it performs as such. I own both the Go 2 and the Pro 2, and the Go 2 brings everything I need to the table. It weighs less, is smaller, and yet still hits hard enough for everyday recovery. Any casual gym-goer or athlete will get all they need from this model (in my experience, that is). That’s why it earned our Best Overall Massage Gun in our guide the best massage guns.

That said, it’s not featherweight. At just over a pound, you’ll notice it in your backpack or gym bag. And because of its traditional L-shaped build, it’s less packable than the Theragun Mini. Good luck squeezing it into a purse or fanny pack.

Still, for the price, the Go 2 punches above its weight class. It’s powerful, user-friendly, and comes with effective attachments. Unless you’re chasing maximum amplitude or boasting bodybuilding levels of muscle mass, it’s all you’ll need.

The Theragun Mini is Therabody’s most affordable and portable massage gun, and arguably its most practical. At half the price of the flagship Theragun Pro, the Mini is designed for athletes, lifters, and weekend warriors who want recovery on the go. Toss it in a gym bag, golf bag, or carry-on; it’s built for on-the-go.

Therabody claims the Mini delivers "scientifically-proven benefits" like reduced muscle tension, fast pain relief, and increased relaxation. Based on my experience, it lives up to the hype. I own four percussive devices (the technical term), and the Mini is my go-to for most tasks.

I was skeptical of the triangular design at first. But it fits naturally in my hand and reaches all the same tight spots as a traditionally-shaped massager. It’s also the most bag-friendly model in my rotation, which is why it’s almost always in my Jiu-Jitsu or gym bag for a quick warm-up.

And despite its size, the Mini packs a powerful punch. It delivers the same top-end percussion per minute as the Theragun Pro and Prime models. You lose a few millimeters of amplitude, which technically means a shallower treatment. In practice, though, I barely noticed the difference.

Head-to-Head Comparison

Portability

Both the Hypervolt Go 2 Theragun Mini are billed as each brand’s most space-saving models, and both live up to the claim. But when it comes to true portability, the Mini edges out the win.

The Go 2 is about 0.7 pounds heavier than the Mini, but I can barely tell. The Go 2’s longer body helps distribute its weight more evenly, while the Mini’s tight build makes it register as denser in hand. Whether I was holding them or tossing them in a bag, the difference was negligible.

The real deciding factor for portability is the shape. The Mini’s triangular form means it’s shockingly low-bulk. I've slipped it into a fanny pack, a couple of my wife’s purses, and even a pair of sweatpants with deep pockets. The Go 2, with its classic gun shape, just doesn’t store the same way. It’ll fit in a larger purse, but not in pockets.

Most people will keep this tool in a gym bag or carry-on. However, if you’re counting every inch of space, the Mini is hands-down the more portable of the two.

Performance

While Hyperice doesn’t list official specs like amplitude or percussions per minute (PPM) for the Go 2 (something it doesn't disclose for any of its models), it's generally believed that the Go 2 is between 2,200 and 3,200 PPM based on testing by multiple outlets. And even based on the intensity level, it puts the Go 2 is in the same ballpark as the Theragun Mini, which clocks in at 12mm of amplitude and a range of 1,750 to 2,400 PPM.

I’ve tested a handful of massage guns from top-tier brands, and here’s what I tell people: As long as they fall within the standard range—roughly 10 to 16mm of amplitude and 1,500 to 2,500 PPM—you’re probably not going to detect much distinction between devices.

That's true here. Both the Go 2 and the Mini offer three speed settings, comparable battery life, and a similar treatment experience. Unless you’re looking for max depth and intensity (think Theragun Pro levels), either model will deliver all the relief and recovery power most people want.

Attachments

Both brands offer comparable heads: A dampener, which is broader and thicker for a less intrusive knead, and a thumb-like attachment to help you dig deep into muscle tissue. (The Mini includes a third “ball” attachment for “all-over therapy”.) Overall, I prefer the Mini’s dampener and the Go 2’s bullet head.

The Dampener Attachment

Hypervolt’s dampener, which Hyperice calls a “flat” head, is shaped like a plate—wide and with an edge. There were times my skin got caught between the edge and the barrel of the massage gun if I held it at too steep an angle. That doesn’t feel good, but as long as you make a point to keep it straight-on, you should be fine. This attachment also has more flat surface area than the Mini’s dampener, resulting in a more vibratory stroke.

[Hypervolt Go](https://memorycircuits.hbsnzj.com/)

The Mini’s dampener, by contrast, resembles a ball that tapers into a broader, hill-shaped top. I used both models on my thighs simultaneously, and they felt pretty similar. That said, I could target more specific areas of my leg with the Mini’s dampener, probably due to the more concentrated, rounded-off shape of its dampener. And while there’s technically still space between the Mini’s dampener and the barrel, I never pinched myself using it.

The Bullet Attachment

The Theragun Mini’s “thumb” attachment shares a similar build to its dampener—broad at the base, tapering into a rounded dome. It’s firm enough to target tight knots and specific muscle areas, but forgiving enough that even massage-gun rookies shouldn't wince. That said, it still delivers a concentrated hit, so use caution when applying it to smaller, more sensitive muscles like your biceps, triceps, or forearms.

The Hypervolt Go 2’s “bullet” is sharper by design, and you feel it; It delivers a deeper, more intense pressing motion that’s better suited for breaking up dense tissue or zeroing in on stubborn trigger points. When I tested both guns side by side on my legs, the Mini felt more surface-level and vibratory, while the Go 2 delivered deeper muscle penetration. Bonus points to Hyperice: The bullet sits within the barrel, which eliminates any risk of pinching your skin during use.

Final Verdict

When it comes to massage quality, the Theragun Mini and Hypervolt Go 2 are virtually indistinguishable. Both deliver a solid massage and similar speed settings. Where they diverge is in form and price.

The Mini is more compact, easier to pack, and slightly more comfortable to hold. It will, however, run you $220. The Go 2, on the other hand, is bulkier and a touch more awkward to travel with, but it’s also $80 cheaper.

If portability is your top priority and you don’t mind paying for it, the Mini is the better fit. But if you’re looking for a dependable, travel-ready massage gun that gets the job done without breaking the bank, the Go 2 is hard to beat. It really comes down to what matters more: The convenience of a smaller footprint or the value of a lower price.
