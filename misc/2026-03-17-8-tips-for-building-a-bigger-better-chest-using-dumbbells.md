# 8 Tips for Building a Bigger, Better Chest Using Dumbbells

Some guys think the only way to a big, broad chest is to use a barbell for heavy bench presses. This is misguided thinking, and you have more tools in your arsenal for chest gains.

In his latest Athlean-X video, Jeff Cavaliere C.S.C.S. shares his best advice for maximizing those chest gains during dumbbell workouts, and offers up eight ways in which adapting your grip, changing techniques, or including specific exercises into your routine can have a big impact.

"Minor tweaks are enough to give you a better chest contraction," he says, "and when you add them up rep after rep after rep, you see the gains start to come."

Drive With Your Legs

When performing a dumbbell bench press, you still need to include leg drive when moving the weight; when your feet are on the floor, you want to be pushing not just down, but also out as if you were sliding your body up the bench. "Drive through the feet and push up to maximize the amount of weight you can lift on the exercise, and therefore the amount of gains you'll get from doing it," says Cavaliere.

Hold Your Weight Right

When using a barbell, our hands and wrists don't have independent function, but when we're lifting dumbbells, this is something we can take advantage of. "If you want more chest contraction, find a way to get more relative adduction," he says.

By altering the position of the dumbbell in your hand and leading with your thumbs rather than your pinkie fingers, you bring your hands closer together, moving the dumbbells further.

Flip Your Grip

If you don't have access to an incline bench, you can still get the benefits on a flat bench by flipping your grip with the underhand grip dumbbell bench press. This move gives you an opportunity to move lower and get your elbows underneath the body, and also to go higher in the movement. "This will always give you a better activation of the chest, only slightly less than the incline bench itself," says Cavaliere.

Start Using Drop Sets

Incorporating drop sets into your routine can make a huge difference in your chest workouts, and is much quicker and easier to do with dumbbells than with a barbell. "It's that to and through failure approach," says Cavaliere. "And always remember you've got that ace in the hole; the ability to drop to the floor, use your own bodyweight, and do a set of pushups to failure."

Use Static Presses

Cavaliere recommends lifting one dumbbell at a time on the bench, then holding them both in the lower end of the movement in a static dumbbell press. "This exposes any unilateral weaknesses you might have that might get traditionally hidden by a fixed hand placement on a barbell," he says. "You've got to make sure you're correcting any of those right and left side imbalances, so by lifting one dumbbell at a time you're going to be able to do that."

Use Dumbbell Pullovers

One dumbbell exercise which is often overlooked as a chest-builder is the dumbbell pullover.

While the traditional version of this move is driven by the lats, Cavaliere demonstrates a variation in the video which brings the elbows together and drives the motion with the upper chest. "It's a move that requires a little bit of practice but as soon as you get it and you feel it, you'll realize just how powerful it is for getting that upper chest carved out."

Use the UCV Raise

A move which maximizes potential for contraction and adduction is the UCV dumbbell raise, performed against an incline bench (or anything you can lean on). For this exercise, Cavaliere recommends using slightly lighter weights for this than other movements. "You're looking for quality contraction, it's not always about loading up when you're looking for chest development."

Use the Chest Fly on the Floor

Conversely, Cavaliere advises loading up on the chest fly, but performing it while lying flat, as a "floor fly", to ensure your shoulders are protected.

"Don't always look at the fly as an exercise that has to be done light," he says. "You have the opportunity to do it with heavier weights, and better gains await you when you do."
