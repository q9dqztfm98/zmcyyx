How to Get the Most Out of Your Protein Powder

When to Take your Protein Powder

Having identified the best type of protein powder for bodybuilders, we next need to work out when is the best time to take it. For many years, it was believed that there was an anabolic window of between 20 and 30 minutes straight after your workout when you have to get your protein in. The belief was that your muscles are crying out for amino acids at that time in order to repair the muscle stress that has occurred during your workout. However, recent studies have debunked this myth.

The renewed understanding is that, provided that you had a pre-workout meal that contains a decent amount of protein, there is no need to rush to take in more protein immediately after your workout. Of course, if you go into the workout in a fasted state (such as when intermittent fasting), then you will want to take in a decent amount of protein within the hour after your workout.

The consensus from the research is that there is no need to worry about timing your protein intake around your workouts. So long as you are regularly taking protein with each of your meals (you should be getting 20 to 30 g of protein at each feeding), then you will be getting sufficient protein to repair and rebuild from your workout. That being said, however, there is nothing wrong with taking a post workout protein shake – it’s just that it is not essential.

As we mentioned in the previous section, you should get into the habit of taking a casein protein powder shake before going to bed. This will provide you with a steady supply of amino acids throughout the night and help to boost your body’s natural release of testosterone and hGH. You should take 40 g of casein protein each evening.

How Much Protein to Take

There has been a lot of debate about how much protein bodybuilders should take each day in order to maximise their muscle growth potential. The current consensus, based on research, is that taking 20 to 25 g of whey protein powder as part of a shake is about ideal to stimulate muscle growth. However, a recent study from 2016 suggests that a higher dosage may be more beneficial. In this study, participants who took 40 g of whey protein powder in their shake had greater muscle gains than a group who took just 20 g. Therefore, we recommend taking anywhere between 20 and 40 g per dosage.

So, how often should you take a protein shake throughout the day. This depends on what your total daily protein requirement is. You should always prioritise whole foods over protein supplements. Concentrate on getting your protein from eggs, chicken, beef, and fish.

You should aim to take in between 0.73 and 1 g of protein per pound of body weight. So, if you are working at the higher end of this scale and you weigh 180 pounds, you will be wanting to take in 180 g of protein per day. You should look to take about 120 g of that through whole food sources. That leaves 60 g to come from your protein shakes, which would equate to 2 shakes at a dosage of 30 g of protein per shake.

You do not want to take in too much protein. Excess protein can be metabolised into glucose and either used for energy or stored as excess body fat. That’s the last thing that any serious bodybuilder wants.

How Protein Builds Muscle

When you work out, you are damaging your muscle cells. This causes micro tears in your muscles that needs to be repaired. The amino acids that you get from protein are the material that is used for that repair work. When you consume protein, your body breaks it down into its constituent amino acid parts. They then form into new proteins that are transported to your muscle cells in order to repair the micro tears that have occurred as a result of your training.

The damage that is taking place to your muscle fibres also stimulates what are called satellite cells. These satellite cells flood the damaged area where they multiply and fuse onto the damaged muscle fibres. In this way they repair the tears, making the muscle bigger and stronger.

This muscle repair strengthening and building process will only take place if you have a sufficient amount of the amino acids to create the proteins that are the building blocks of this whole process. In fact, you want enough protein coming into your system that there are actually excess amino acids that can be used to further muscle growth.

If you do not provide your body with a sufficient amount of protein, the damaged cells will not be able to fully repair your muscles and, as a result of that, you will end up weaker and smaller than you were before you worked out. In fact, in the absence of protein, your muscles are likely to start kept catabolising, or eating away at themselves.
