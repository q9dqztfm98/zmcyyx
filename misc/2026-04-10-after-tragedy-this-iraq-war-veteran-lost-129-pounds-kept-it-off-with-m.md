# After Tragedy, This Iraq War Veteran Lost 129 Pounds & Kept It Off With Music

Sal Gonzalez, 40, of Murfreesboro, TN was inspired to join the US Marine Corps because of the 9/11 terrorist attacks. He was deployed to Iraq, where he was hit by an improvised explosive device (IED) and experienced a traumatic brain injury. He later dealt with PTSD and the amputation of his left leg below the knee–but Sal wouldn’t give up.

[This Iraq War](https://thymosin-report.hbsnzj.com/blog/0688818794.html)

I HAD TO lose 20 pounds just to join the Marines when I enlisted. I ran my butt off, and only ate salad and chicken for weeks. I still didn’t lose enough, so I cut the last few pounds with a laxative pill called Ex-Lax. It was fairly easy to maintain my weight once I was in the Marines, but at 210 pounds on a 5’9 frame, I was still heavier than I should have been by Marine standards.

After I was hit with an IED while deployed in Iraq, I spent a week in a coma and after that, I couldn't eat solid food. I was pretty miserable, because food is happiness for me. Even when I could eat, I could only have small portions of bad hospital food every other day because I was in and out of 37 surgeries.

My weight started getting bad when I was medically retired from the Marine Corps at 25. I kept eating like an infantryman, but I quit exercising because I didn’t need to be in shape anymore. My weight crept up to 299 pounds. My diet was no better. It revolved around pizza, bread, fried foods, soda, and booze. At my heaviest, I was tired all the time and the weight I carried was hard on my prosthetic leg.

Making Slow, Steady Changes

It took a very long time for me to get my stuff together. From previous experience, I knew I would fail and quit if I tried to get in shape by going full bore. Around 2011, I started walking more regularly. At first, I could only go a couple of hundred yards before my leg started to hurt, but I didn’t stop. Every day, every week, I went a little further.

Soon after, I was introduced to cycling. I attended a Wounded Warrior Project Soldier Ride event, which brings veterans together to ride bikes as a unit, regardless of their injury or experience level. Soldier Ride’s motto is “we ride together, we finish together” and the camaraderie made the experience a lot of fun.

Walking and cycling soon turned into jogging. I got to the point where I could jog a mile without stopping. After six months, I’d lost 49 pounds. I felt better than I had in years and I kept the momentum going. Since I did judo as a kid, I also enrolled at my local MMA school and earned a blue belt in Brazilian jiu-jitsu.

By the time I got married and had a son, my weight yo-yoed between 240 and 220 pounds. I wanted to be able to run around with my kid, so I took exercising even more seriously. I bought a bike and a baby trailer to ride around. From that point on, if I wasn’t riding my actual bike, I would be riding my wife’s Peloton.

Focusing on Food

In 2023, Wounded Warrior Project’s Physical Health & Wellness program helped me find a nutritionist, and I learned how to count calories. I started tracking my food like a madman! I used a scale and the Lose It app to track every morsel of food that went into my mouth down to the exact calorie. I mean everything—from a single gummy bear from my kids to a scheduled meal. If it went in my gut, it went on the app. Sometimes I’d break out my phone and start typing into the app right as I was bringing the food up to my mouth!

When I was the most strict with my diet, I found a great hack to help with late-night food cravings. I would eat frozen grapes mixed with sugar-free Jello powder and some lemon juice and I still eat them more than ice cream to this day! Grapes are a great substitute when dieting but sometimes there aren't any easy substitutes. Case in point: Cauliflower pizza sucks. So instead, I’m at a two-slice limit instead of eating the whole pie.

Over the course of about a year, I got down to 170 pounds, with around 12 percent body fat. And while I don’t track my meals anymore, I know what portions are supposed to look like. That intuition has served me well. Overall, I lost 129 pounds and today, I’m around 195 pounds.

Setting Goals and Making Music

I try to excel in everything I do–not because I want to be the best, but because it makes me happy to try. I find that I maintain motivation for mundane workouts by giving myself a skill goal.

Currently, I’m working on muscle-ups. I’ve never been great at pull-ups, and can only do five right now, but working toward that muscle-up goal keeps me wanting to get back on the bar.

My music is another big motivator for maintaining a healthy lifestyle. Last summer, I made my debut at the Grand Ole Opry (country music’s biggest stage), and I have a self-titled EP coming out on November 11. When I’m exercising regularly, eating well, and getting my therapy via music, I feel my best. My brain feels like it’s firing on all cylinders, which is a feat due to my traumatic brain injury.

My advice to anyone wanting to lose weight would be to just start moving. Also, don’t be too hard on yourself. One bad day doesn’t undo a good week. It’s all about getting back on that horse, and stacking up good weeks to live a healthier life.
