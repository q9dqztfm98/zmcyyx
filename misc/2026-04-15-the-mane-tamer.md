# The Mane Tamer

"Barbershops started dying in the 1970s," says Shorty Maniace, head barber at Manhattan's FSC Barber. "They became chop shops, factories. But a good haircut is art. I might not be able to create a Pollock, but I can create a memorable haircut."

Maniace's customers want to stand out subtly, and when they step through the door of FSC Barber they're immediately surrounded by details of a more demure era. The soft glow of the shop's art-deco light fixtures plays off the subway tile-clad walls. Plush barber's chairs from the '20s and '30s provide welcome perches.

But the period feel is more than just cosmetic. Maniace, 37, scours eBay and antique stores for functioning tools, from his Stim-U-Lax Junior face massager to a windup razor. His pièce de resistance is a set of 1940s manual clippers that can churn out the perfectly smooth neck fades of vintage Hollywood. After each snip, he repositions them with just the right amount of hair in the jaws, and then neatly shears the excess free. Rise. Repeat. Review. And so on. "They give me more control," he says.

Don't Be a Burn VictimEarlobes are a poor measuring stick for self-administered sideburn trims. A better technique: Cut one sideburn and then hold a pocket comb to the bottom of your jaw, noting which tooth it reaches. Move the comb to the other side of your face, and trim at the same tooth.

Draw the LineNeck fuzz grows faster than head hair. Trim your own by feeling for the base of your hairline and positioning the trimmers flat across and directly below that line. Slowly slide down and away from the hairline. Never mow upward—that could disturb your hair's normal growth pattern.
