# Alcohol & Sleepwalking: The Dangerous Link (Doctor’s Warning) | Ubie Doctor's Note

Published on: 7/21/2026

How does alcohol consumption affect sleepwalking?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Alcohol can worsen sleepwalking by disrupting normal brain activity during sleep, which may trigger unusual nighttime behaviors such as walking, talking, or performing complex actions while unconscious. Because alcohol suppresses REM sleep and increases deep non-REM sleep—the stage when sleepwalking typically occurs—it raises the likelihood and intensity of episodes, especially in people already prone to parasomnias.

If you or a loved one are experiencing sleepwalking, don't guess at the cause or wait to see if it resolves on its own. Underlying triggers can range from alcohol use and stress to sleep disorders or medication side effects—each requiring a different approach. Explanation

Alcohol can have a significant impact on sleepwalking by altering brain activity during sleep. According to the research, alcohol consumption can lead to unusual sleep behaviors, possibly by affecting the REM (Rapid Eye Movement) sleep phase. This phase is crucial for restful sleep, and disturbances in it can lead to disorders like sleepwalking.

REM Sleep and Alcohol: Alcohol can disrupt REM sleep, leading to behaviors that are not typical during normal sleep cycles. This disruption can increase the likelihood of sleepwalking episodes.
