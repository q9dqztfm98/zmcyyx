How does alcohol consumption affect urine output?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Alcohol makes you pee more because it suppresses vasopressin, a hormone that helps your kidneys retain water. With less vasopressin, your kidneys release more fluid into your bladder, leading to frequent urination and potential dehydration.

If frequent urination is bothering you—whether tied to drinking or not—it may signal something worth understanding. Explanation

Alcohol consumption can lead to increased urine output because it affects the body's fluid balance. When you drink alcohol, it can interfere with the hormone that helps your body hold onto water. This hormone is called antidiuretic hormone (ADH). Without enough ADH, your body doesn't hold onto water as well, so you end up peeing more.

Here's how it works:

Alcohol blocks ADH: When alcohol is in your system, it can block the release of ADH.

[Stop How Alcohol Affects](https://antimicrobial-hub.sunsimiaosd.com/)

Less ADH means more pee: With less ADH, your kidneys don't hold onto water as much, so you make more urine.

Dehydration risk: Because you're peeing more, you might get dehydrated if you don't drink enough water.

It's important to remember that staying hydrated is key. If you're drinking alcohol, make sure to also drink water to help keep your body in balance.
