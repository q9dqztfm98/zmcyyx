Explanation

In recent years, General Practitioners (GPs) have increasingly integrated glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1 RAs) into routine care. GLP-1 usage in primary care is on the rise because these medications offer a powerful combination of blood sugar control, weight management and cardiovascular protection. Below, we'll explore what GLP-1s are, why your GP might suggest them, and how they fit into a broader care plan—without glossing over what you need to know.

What Are GLP-1 Receptor Agonists?

Glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) is a naturally occurring gut hormone (an incretin) that helps regulate glucose and appetite. GLP-1 receptor agonists are synthetic versions that mimic this hormone to:

Boost glucose-dependent insulin secretion

Suppress inappropriate glucagon release

Slow gastric emptying, increasing satiety

Reduce appetite and caloric intake

Commonly prescribed GLP-1 RAs in primary care include:

Liraglutide

Semaglutide

Dulaglutide

Exenatide

These drugs are endorsed by leading bodies such as the American Diabetes Association (ADA), European Association for the Study of Diabetes (EASD) and the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) for their benefits in type 2 diabetes and obesity management.

Why Your GP Recommends GLP-1s

1. Improved Glycemic Control

Average HbA1c reductions of 1.0%–1.5% when added to metformin or other oral agents

Low risk of hypoglycemia because insulin release is glucose-dependent

2. Weight Management

Sustained weight loss of 5–15% of body weight in many patients

Useful for people with type 2 diabetes who also struggle with overweight or obesity

3. Cardiovascular Benefits

Reduction in major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) by 10–20% in high-risk patients

Proven benefits in patients with established heart disease

4. Potential Renal Protection

Slowing of diabetic kidney disease progression has been observed

Particularly beneficial for patients with early signs of nephropathy

Who Is a Good Candidate?

GLP-1 usage in primary care focuses on patients who:

Have type 2 diabetes not controlled by metformin and lifestyle changes

Struggle with overweight (BMI ≥27 kg/m²) or obesity (BMI ≥30 kg/m²) with comorbidities

Have established cardiovascular disease or high CV risk

Are unable to tolerate or have contraindications to other antihyperglycemic drugs

Initial Evaluation

Before starting a GLP-1 RA, your GP will typically:

Review your medical history (especially GI disorders, personal/family history of thyroid carcinoma or multiple endocrine neoplasia type 2)

Check kidney and liver function

Confirm baseline HbA1c, weight and BMI

Discuss cost, insurance coverage and patient assistance programs

GI side effects are common, especially early on:

Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea

Abdominal pain

Injection site reactions

Strategies to improve tolerance:

Slow titration: Increase the dose in small increments

Dietary adjustments: Eat smaller, more frequent meals; avoid high-fat foods

Stay hydrated and consider separate sips of water between meals

Safety and Contraindications

While generally well tolerated, GLP-1 RAs are not suitable for everyone. Contraindications include:

Personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma or MEN 2

History of pancreatitis (use with caution and close monitoring)

Pregnancy and breastfeeding (insufficient safety data)

Severe gastrointestinal disease (e.g., gastroparesis)

Your GP will weigh these factors and choose the most appropriate therapy.

Integrating GLP-1s Into Holistic Care

Lifestyle modification: Nutrition counseling, physical activity, sleep hygiene

Cardiovascular risk management: Blood pressure control, lipid-lowering therapy

Behavioral support: Referrals to dietitians, diabetes educators, mental health professionals
