Why Your Doctor Warns About Mixing Shilajit and Blood Thinners

Shilajit is a popular supplement in traditional Ayurvedic medicine. Made from decomposed plant material found in high-altitude rocks, it's rich in fulvic acid, minerals and antioxidants. While many people take shilajit for energy and overall wellness, those on anticoagulant (blood thinner) therapy need to be cautious. Here's what you should know about shilajit and anticoagulant safety.

A sticky, tar-like substance used for centuries in Ayurvedic practice

Contains fulvic and humic acids, trace minerals (iron, zinc, magnesium) and various bioactive compounds

Clinical trials on shilajit are limited. Most safety and efficacy data come from small studies or laboratory research. That means unexpected interactions can arise when it's paired with prescription medications, especially blood thinners.

How Blood Thinners Work

Anticoagulants help prevent dangerous blood clots, reducing your risk of stroke, heart attack or pulmonary embolism. Common prescription blood thinners include:

Heparin (typically administered in hospital settings)

These drugs work by interfering with clot-forming factors in your blood. Their activity must be carefully monitored to keep you within a safe therapeutic range. Too little effect allows clots to form; too much effect raises your risk of bleeding.

Potential Interactions Between Shilajit and Anticoagulants

Research on shilajit's interaction with blood thinners is sparse. However, several mechanisms raise concerns:

Mineral content: Shilajit contains minerals like iron and calcium that might influence vitamin K–dependent clotting factors, potentially reducing warfarin's effectiveness.

Fulvic acid properties: Fulvic acid may chelate certain drugs or alter their absorption in the gut.

Enzyme modulation: Some herbal compounds affect liver enzymes (CYP450 family) that metabolize anticoagulants, leading to unpredictable drug levels.

Variability in purity: Over-the-counter shilajit products can be contaminated with heavy metals or other herbs, introducing additional interaction risks.

Because of these factors, combining shilajit with blood thinners can lead to either reduced anticoagulant effect (raising clot risk) or enhanced effect (raising bleeding risk).

Risks of Mixing Shilajit and Blood Thinners

Even if you don't experience immediate issues, the long-term combination of shilajit and anticoagulants may:

Cause uncontrolled bleeding—from minor bruises to serious internal hemorrhage

Lead to clot formation if shilajit decreases anticoagulant levels

Interfere with routine blood tests like INR (International Normalized Ratio) for warfarin monitoring

Trigger unexpected side effects due to herbal contaminants

Who's Most at Risk?

People on warfarin (narrow therapeutic index, requires strict monitoring)

Patients taking multiple medications that affect blood clotting

Individuals with liver or kidney impairment (affects drug clearance)

Older adults or anyone prone to falls and injuries
