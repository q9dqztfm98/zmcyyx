What Is a Specialized Wound Care Center?

A specialized wound care center is a medical facility focusing exclusively on the assessment, diagnosis, and treatment of acute and chronic wounds. These centers typically include:

Multidisciplinary Teams: Wound care specialists, vascular surgeons, endocrinologists (for diabetics), podiatrists, nurses, dietitians and physical therapists

Advanced Therapies: Negative pressure wound therapy (NPWT), hyperbaric oxygen therapy, bioengineered skin substitutes, growth factors

Diagnostic Tools: Doppler ultrasound for blood flow, imaging (X-ray, MRI), lab tests for infection markers

Why Your Doctor May Refer You

Physicians refer patients to a wound care center when standard wound management isn't sufficient or when specialized expertise is needed. Common reasons include:

Diabetic Foot Ulcers: Reduced blood flow and nerve damage can slow healing and increase infection risk

Pressure Injuries: Bedsores or pressure ulcers that worsen despite repositioning and basic dressings

Venous Leg Ulcers: Vein valve failure leading to persistent leg or ankle wounds

Preparing for Your Visit

To make the most of your appointment:

Bring all current medications (including insulin, oral agents, supplements).

Pack recent blood sugar records and any imaging or lab results.

Wear loose, comfortable clothing that allows easy access to the wound area.

Monitoring Progress at Home

Between visits, you'll likely need to:

Change dressings as instructed, keeping the wound clean and dry.

Inspect your wound daily, noting size, color, drainage or odor changes.

Maintain optimal blood sugar levels to support healing.

Follow dietary and exercise recommendations to boost circulation.

Report any new pain, redness, swelling, or fever to your care team immediately.

When to Seek Urgent Medical Attention

While wound care centers greatly reduce risks, certain signs warrant immediate medical care:

Increased redness or warmth around the wound

Uncontrolled bleeding or sudden enlargement of the wound

Foul-smelling or greenish drainage

New or worsening pain despite pain management

High fever (above 100.4°F/38°C) or chills

Numbness spreading beyond the wound site

If you experience any of these symptoms, call your wound care team or emergency services right away.

Final Thoughts

A referral to a specialized wound care center for diabetics is a proactive step to protect your health, preserve mobility and prevent serious complications such as infection or amputation.
