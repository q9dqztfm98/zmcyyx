What is TMG?

TMG is trimethylglycine, also known as betaine. It’s a naturally occurring compound in animals (including humans), microorganisms, and plants. Common dietary sources include beets, spinach, wheat bran, wheat germ, dairy, eggs, chicken, and fish.15 Whether taken through food or supplements, it yields a similar bioavailability.16

Insider Tip: Betaine is pronounced “BEE-ta-een” (not “buh-TAYN” or “BEH-ta-een”). Its name stems from its initial discovery in sugar beets.

In the body, TMG is both an osmolyte and a methyl group donor. As an osmolyte, it helps regulate the balance of water and salt in cells, so it’s essential for maintaining a stable cellular environment, allowing cells to function as they should.12 As a methyl group donor, it readily gives up one or more of its methyl groups to other compounds in a process called methylation, which is integral to gene expression (by which the information encoded into a gene becomes a function).17

TMG’s role as a methyl group donor specifically relates to an amino acid called homocysteine. Homocysteine isn’t necessarily good or bad. It’s normal to have some of it in your blood. But when you have elevated levels, it can cause health problems like coronary artery disease, heart attack, stroke, and cognitive decline.18 B vitamins can break it down into chemicals your body needs, but not effectively if you have a vitamin deficiency or a defect in the gene (MTHFR) responsible for processing it.19

That’s where TMG can intervene. It too breaks down homocysteine, specifically into the essential antioxidant amino acid methionine, which is necessary for making protein in your body and regulating metabolic processes.20 21 So, it effectively rehabilitates a delinquent compound into a contributing member to your biochemistry.

Primary types and benefits of TMG supplements

On the supplements market, you’ll find TMG supplements in two forms: TMG hydrochloride (HCl) and TMG anhydrous.

In the HCl form, TMG is attached to hydrochloric acid. Its principal use is to raise stomach acid levels to help you break down protein, absorb nutrients from food, and control the levels of pathogens in your digestive tract — functions that are compromised in certain health conditions (atrophic gastritis or Helicobacter pylori infection) or with the use of acid-reducing medications (antacids, H2 receptor blockers, or proton pump inhibitors).22

TMG anhydrous, in contrast, is nonacidic. Think of it as TMG Classic, the fundamental form that functions as an osmolyte and methyl group donor. As such, it can benefit you primarily in these areas:

Fluid balance

Osmolytes regulate osmosis, the passage of water through semipermeable membranes such as cell walls. The law of osmosis dictates that water will move from a region of lower solute concentration to a region of higher solute concentration, equalizing the concentrations on both sides of the membrane. This fluid balance is necessary for cells to retain optimal size and function. Because TMG is an osmolyte, it brings water into the cell and takes it out as needed, which in turn helps counteract heat stress and potentially combats the visible effects of skin aging related to hydration.23 24

Physical strength

Methionine is a precursor of creatine, a compound that increases your body’s ability to regenerate adenosine triphosphate (ATP).27 28 ATP is the primary carrier of cellular energy, so an increase in available ATP corresponds to a higher capacity for physical strength performance.29 And because higher levels of TMG lead to larger volumes of circulating methionine, it follows that TMG supplementation may increase your strength potential via elevated serum creatine.

Such increases have been observed in a few studies. For example, a 2011 study saw moderate increases in bench press repetitions and volume load with 2.5g of TMG per day;30 a 2022 study on collegiate athletes reported improved upper body and maximal strength in weight workouts with 5g per day;27 and a 2024 systematic review concluded that “[TMG] supplementation for at least seven days significantly enhances muscular strength, especially lower body strength, and shows potential in improving vertical jumping performance.”14

NAD+ production

TMG works within the NAD+ production pipeline to support nicotinamide (NAM) in its transformation into nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN). NMN goes on to become nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+), which is important for cellular health and lifespan. NAD+, for its part, breaks back down into NAM and begins that cycle anew. So TMG plays a key role in a process that can preserve proper cellular function, itself a factor in longevity.42

Other potential benefits of TMG

A more limited body of research suggests that TMG may play a role in other areas of health, as well, though the findings aren’t conclusive:

Cardiovascular health

TMG’s implications for cardiovascular health relate to its action on homocysteine. In a 2003 study, human subjects who received 1.5g, 3g, or 6g of supplemental TMG over six weeks exhibited decreases in their homocysteine levels by 12%, 15%, and 20%, respectively, compared to placebo.25 Another study, the following year, yielded comparable outcomes with roughly similar doses over four weeks.26 Tying these studies together, a 2024 review and meta-analysis concluded that 4g of TMG per day could positively affect cardiovascular markers via homocysteine decrease without adverse effects on lipid profiles.9

At the same time, we have to consider that doses greater than 4g per day have demonstrated adverse effects on lipid profiles, namely by increasing levels of low-density lipoprotein (LDL) cholesterol (i.e., “bad” cholesterol).9 Therefore, one must carefully balance their daily TMG intake against cardiovascular risks.

Chronic disease management

Recent studies have described TMG as an exercise mimetic, or a substance that mimics some of the benefits of exercise, which is essential to overall health. A 2025 study of mice, for example, notes that TMG may exert “multi-organ geroprotection,”47 and a 2026 review states that it has exhibited “significant preventive and therapeutic effects in studies on chronic diseases.”48

It can’t be said conclusively that TMG can truly slow the progression of chronic diseases in humans, but perhaps it may contribute in small ways to this effect.

Theoretically, since homocysteine is also linked to cognitive decline, TMG supplementation should also have protective action against neurodegenerative disorders such as Alzheimer’s, but theory doesn’t quite play out in practice. For example, in 2001, a 24-week pilot study on Alzheimer’s patients ended up with variable results that couldn’t conclusively be interpreted to support TMG’s use as a cognitive protectant.31

More convincing is TMG’s potential to prevent or attenuate liver disorders, but even then the support is limited and would seem to involve very high doses (e.g., 10g per day) that are impractical from a supplementation standpoint and unsafe in terms of cardiovascular risk.32 33

Are TMG supplements safe?

Apart from the unknown side effect profile for people who are pregnant or breastfeeding, the biggest safety issue with TMG supplementation is the risk of elevated cholesterol,32 though the risk seems to present itself only with doses exceeding 4g per day.9 The label-recommended doses of our top-recommended TMG supplements are significantly lower than 4g, so you ought to be fine taking any of them as directed — unless you already have a high or borderline-high lipid profile.

That’s without accounting for other ingredients in a given supplement, which might carry separate risks. We discuss potentially concerning safety issues of individual TMG supplements in their respective brand sections later on.

TMG HCl, however, may pose a risk to people with peptic ulcers. Because it raises stomach acid levels, it could exacerbate ulcers or prevent their healing.43

Who should (or shouldn’t) take a TMG supplement?

As long as you’re not pregnant or lactating and you don’t have high cholesterol, a TMG supplement could be a sensible addition to your supplement regimen if you want to:

Improve your physical strength performance

Lower your cardiovascular risk (notwithstanding the previously mentioned risks)

Extend your longevity in general

But don’t expect that taking TMG will automatically produce these outcomes. By itself, TMG isn’t going to make you into some mega-muscled Methuselah. At best, it’s a support piece in an ecosystem of healthy living that includes exercise, a sensible diet, regular healthcare, and other proactive measures.

Also, despite claims to the contrary, you shouldn’t expect a TMG supplement to net you any appreciable benefits to your cognitive or liver health. Studies on TMG’s neuroprotective potential have been limited in scope and mixed in their findings, while its liver-protective effects require doses that are both higher than what you can reasonably get from a supplement and possibly harmful to your cardiovascular health.

What about TMG HCl?

TMG HCl serves a different function, so it’s best discussed separately.

This variant of TMG supplements is suited to people with indigestion stemming from insufficient production of hydrochloric acid, the main component of stomach acid. Called hypochlorhydria, this health condition is more common in older people, those with chronic stress, and patients who’ve undergone stomach surgery.22

Though there’s evidence of its ability to improve symptoms of hypochlorhydric indigestion, TMG HCl is more of a temporary relief protocol than a cure.44 Moreover, it isn’t an appropriate intervention for people with peptic ulcers, as the increase in stomach acid levels can irritate an ulcer or prevent it from healing, and it’s contraindicated for people taking medication for decreasing stomach acid, like proton pump inhibitors (they’re taking the medication for a reason, and TMG HCl could undo the benefits).43

TMG may be a streamlined channel toward achieving physical strength and cardiovascular outcomes, but it’s not necessarily the best or only option for either one. Below are some alternatives for those health goals, as well as a couple for the digestive improvements you might seek with TMG HCl.

For physical strength performance

For cardiovascular health

TMG supplements FAQ

What are TMG supplements good for?

The best-supported uses of TMG supplements are for improving cardiovascular health,25 26 9 physical strength performance,27 30 and cellular fluid balance (the last of these may help counteract heat stress and skin aging).23 24 It also acts as an excellent methyl donor in the NAD+ production process.42 Clinical research supports TMG use to support liver health, but with higher doses than you should take.32 Some people claim that it can help with cognitive health and weight loss, but there’s no credible evidence at this time to support those claims.

A separate category of TMG supplements, TMG hydrochloride, is used to raise stomach acid levels, which can help users digest protein, absorb nutrients from food, and control the levels of pathogens in the digestive tract.22

Can TMG improve liver health?

Research supports TMG’s ability to improve liver biomarkers, including in cases of steatosis and fibrosis, but you’d have to take very high doses of 10g per day or more.32 Once you start taking that much, you begin to elevate your risk of elevated cholesterol levels, which is hardly a sensible trade-off.33

Can TMG increase testosterone levels?

A few studies have concluded that it can. For example, in a 2021 study, youth soccer players who took 2g of TMG per day exhibited increased testosterone levels and testosterone-to-cortisol ratios over 14 weeks of supplementation.46

What’s the difference between TMG and betaine?

There’s no difference aside from the name. Sometimes, supplements go by multiple names. Vitamin B9, for example, is also known as folate and was formerly called folacin.
