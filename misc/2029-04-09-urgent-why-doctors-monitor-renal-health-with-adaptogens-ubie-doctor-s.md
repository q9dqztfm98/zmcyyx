Why Your Doctor Monitors Renal Health While You Use Adaptogens

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Adaptogens are processed and eliminated through your kidneys, which is why doctors monitor renal function before and during use. Without proper oversight, active compounds can accumulate, triggering toxin buildup, electrolyte imbalances, and dangerous drug interactions.

Key factors that affect your safety include: who requires extra monitoring (such as those with chronic kidney disease, diabetes, or hypertension), which lab tests to request (creatinine, GFR, BUN, electrolyte panels), warning signs of kidney stress (swelling, changes in urination, fatigue, nausea), and evidence-based safe usage guidelines.

Explanation

Adaptogens are a class of herbs and supplements—such as ashwagandha—that help the body adapt to stress. They've become increasingly popular for managing fatigue, anxiety, and overall well-being. While most people tolerate adaptogens well, your kidneys play a key role in processing and eliminating their active compounds. That's why your doctor may keep a closer eye on your renal health when you start or adjust an adaptogen regimen.

The Kidneys' Essential Role

Your kidneys filter waste products, balance electrolytes, regulate blood pressure, and manage fluid levels. When you take any supplement—including adaptogens—your kidneys help:

Remove metabolic byproducts

Excrete excess minerals and water

Maintain acid-base balance

Process and eliminate herbal compounds

If kidney function declines, toxic substances can build up, potentially causing symptoms like fatigue, nausea, swelling, or more serious complications.

Why Adaptogens Require Caution

Adaptogens like ashwagandha are botanicals with biologically active compounds. Although generally safe, they can:

Interact with prescription medications (e.g., blood pressure drugs, immunosuppressants)

Alter electrolyte balance (particularly sodium and potassium)

Affect blood pressure or blood sugar levels

Pose a theoretical risk of increased kidney workload

Ashwagandha and kidney function are linked because ashwagandha's metabolites are cleared in the urine. In people with already reduced renal function, these compounds can accumulate.

Who Needs Extra Monitoring?

Your doctor will consider closer kidney monitoring if you have one or more of the following:

Chronic kidney disease (CKD) or a history of kidney stones

Diabetes or high blood pressure (both major causes of CKD)

Age over 65 (kidney function naturally declines with age)

Use of multiple medications or supplements that rely on renal clearance

Symptoms such as persistent fatigue, swelling in ankles or face, or changes in urination

Common Tests for Renal Health

To ensure your kidneys handle adaptogens safely, your physician may order:

Serum Creatinine & Estimated Glomerular Filtration Rate (eGFR): Measures filtration efficiency.

Blood Urea Nitrogen (BUN): Indicates waste buildup.

Electrolyte Panel: Checks sodium, potassium, chloride, and bicarbonate levels.

Urinalysis: Detects protein, blood, or other abnormal substances.

These tests establish a baseline and help track any changes over time.

What the Research Says

Limited Cases of Herb-Induced NephropathySome herbal remedies have been implicated in kidney injury—often due to contamination, improper dosing, or interactions. While reports involving adaptogens are rare, vigilance remains important.

Ashwagandha and Electrolyte ShiftsAnimal studies suggest that high doses of ashwagandha extract may affect sodium and potassium levels. In humans, these effects appear mild, but they underscore the need for monitoring, especially in vulnerable patients.

Drug-Herb Interactions

Ashwagandha might enhance the effects of blood thinners and blood pressure medications.

Adjustments in drug dosing may be required to prevent hypotension (low blood pressure) or hyperkalemia (high potassium).

Best Practices for Safe Adaptogen Use

To minimize risk and maximize benefits:

Consult Before You Start

Share your full medical history and current medications.

Discuss any known kidney issues or family history of renal disease.

Start with Low Doses

Gradually increase to the recommended amount.

Monitor how you feel and report any unusual symptoms.

Stay Hydrated

Adequate water intake supports healthy filtration.

Aim for at least 8 glasses of fluid per day, adjusting for activity and climate.

Schedule Periodic Lab Tests

Confirm stable kidney function at baseline and every 3–6 months.

Balancing Benefits and Risks

Ashwagandha and kidney function concerns are generally minimal in healthy adults.

Final Thoughts

Your kidneys work around the clock to keep you healthy. By monitoring renal health through lab tests and clinical check-ins, your doctor ensures that adaptogens like ashwagandha remain a safe tool in your wellness arsenal. With clear communication, appropriate dosing, and routine testing, you can enjoy the stress-resilience benefits of adaptogens without compromising kidney function.
