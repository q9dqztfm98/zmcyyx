# Mike Evans Is the NFL's Most Reliable Receiver. Here's How He Trains for Consistency.

THE MOST CONSISTENT wide receiver in the the NFL isn't the sport's biggest name or loudest personality. Mike Evans, who has been churning out 1,000-yard seasons in Tampa Bay since entering the league in 2014, firmly holds that title.

The 32-year-old Galveston, Texas, native has maintained a level of excellence that no receiver in league history can match to start their career. The 6’5”, 231-pound wideout is the only player in NFL history to record at least 1,000 receiving yards in each of his first 11 NFL seasons (tying Jerry Rice for the total consecutive mark, who posted 1K seasons from 1986 to 1996 after his second year in the league). Evans has been voted to six Pro Bowls, won a Super Bowl title in 2020, and holds Buccaneers franchise records for most touchdown receptions (105), total touchdowns (106), receiving yards (12,684) and receptions (836). Last year, he also passed Randy Moss for the second-most 1,000-yard seasons of all-time, only behind Rice's 14. If Evans remains as consistent in the 2025 season as he has been throughout his career, he'll break Rice's consecutive 1K-yard seasons record and inch closer to his total mark, too.

At a position where off-field eccentricity is almost expected, Evans' approach to life seems to be just as he is on the field: measured and consistent. He coaches his oldest daughter’s basketball team in the off-season and can answer just about any Harry Potter trivia question he's asked—and he's all business when it comes to training.

Earlier in his career, he says he took too much time off in the off-season. Now, he gets back to the grind as soon as he’s home in Texas for the offseason. He had a football field built in his backyard, and he trains there alongside high school and college players and the trainer he's worked with for the last decade. “That's when I can really get some good work in, because they're pushing me,” Evans says of the younger players. “I was once in their shoes, and I know what they're going through. It gives me added motivation.”

He also lends the field out to his trainer whenever he isn’t using it. But once his off-season training ramps up, that isn’t often. A decade in the NFL has taught Evans how important it is to show up every day. “It's simple, but it's hard to do,” he says. “It's the discipline and the consistency. That's what you gotta have.”

For Evans, that means waking up around 6 a.m. and heading out to his backyard field. He’ll move through 90 to 120 minutes of field drills, focusing on explosion, speed, footwork and resistance band runs—ten 30-yard sprints with the band on, 10 with the band off. On days he’s focused on honing football-specific movements like cutting, and he’ll use a VertiMax machine to provide resistance as well.

After field work, Evans lifts for an hour. While he trained with heavy weights in college, Evans says he now focuses on lighter lifts with more reps. On upper body days, he’ll move through a session of dumbbell incline press, hammer curls, pullups, and shoulder presses. On lower body days, he focuses first on his hamstrings with dumbbell RDLs, back squats capped at 225 pounds, dumbbell lunges, and calf raises. “Even though my calves don’t look big, they’re strong,” Evans says.

Evans especially likes core training, focusing on bicycle crunches—a favorite from his days at Texas A&M, where his strength coach would make the players move through them after the hardest workouts—and holding plank variations.

After weight room work, Evans gets a massage. “Shit. I'm trying to do everything,” he says of recovery. That includes targeted cryotherapy, massage, stretching, and more. He recently started implementing a sauna/cold tub contrast regimen, which he cites as making a difference in helping his muscles recover. Recovery isn't all serious focus, either—Evans plays video games while hooked up to a Game Ready machine.

For nutrition, Evans has a chef in Galveston and a chef in Tampa Bay. “I’m very simple and I’m not a picky eater,” Evans says. He loves proteins, seafood with veggies and Caesar salad. He doesn't drink coffee, and sticks to water for hydration.

When he’s not grinding out cardio on the field, Evans loves to box. He started the sport seven years ago, and he’ll box at least twice a week. “It’s great cardio, and it shows you what you’re made of,” he says. He’ll also utilize the pool at his house and run sprints in the water while his kids play. During the season, he runs in the Hydroworx pool at the Buccaneer's facility. “I feel good afterward,” Evans says. “I don't feel like I fucking ran it all, but I feel strong.”

One workout you won’t catch Evans doing? Pilates. He attempted to take classes with his wife a few years ago—but he says mastering the positions is the one physical challenge too difficult for the NFL's most consistent pass catcher. “I’m not that flexible,” he laughs.
