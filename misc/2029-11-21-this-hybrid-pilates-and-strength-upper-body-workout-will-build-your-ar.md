# This Hybrid Pilates And Strength Upper-Body Workout Will Build Your Arms And Shoulders

[Workout Will Build](https://insulin-pathways.hbsnzj.com/blog/2548927629.html)

STRENGTH TRAINING AND Pilates are a potent combo. Strength training moves are often at their best allowing you to push your muscles through large ranges of motion. Pilates, meanwhile, often pushes you to live in smaller ranges of motion (and in doing so, it creates that trademark Pilates burn). The two disciplines can complement each other, building well-rounded strength.

[Workout Will Build](https://cortexcompass.jcesqc8.com/blog/8784145859.html)

Enter: Pilates x Lift, a four-week workout plan led by Nike trainer and Pilates instructor Tara Bethune and fitness trainer and kettlebell expert Marcus Martinez, C.S.C.S. This workout is one of five that you get with the program, and it’s a hybrid upper body workout, fusing elements of Pilates and strength training. You’ll use Pilates to “prime” the muscle groups for the bigger lifts that come later in the workout, Bethune says.

This workout kicks off with a warmup and then moves through four focused blocks. You’ll start with thoracic rotations to prep your back for the core work ahead. As you twist, think about getting taller, keeping your legs grounded, and isolating the upper body for maximum benefit, Bethune says.

Block one emphasizes push motions, beginning with scapular pushups to wake up your shoulders and postural muscles so you’re ready to lift heavier later. Keep a soft bend in your elbows if you tend to hyperextend, and maintain a long neck with your gaze just past your fingertips to keep your spine aligned, she advises.

The pull block comes next, where swan-to-Supermans prime your back with shoulder flexion and retraction. This move sets you up for the mid- and lower-back strength work that follows. After that, the Pilates shoulder primer brings mobility through internal and external rotation. Choose light weights—two to five pounds is plenty—because the small, repetitive movements will set your muscles on fire. The block closes with alternating curls to hit your biceps; avoid swinging your arms and focus on tension to really build muscle.

Finally, the workout wraps with a tough finisher: oblique- and core-focused exercises, followed by burnout pushups to seal the deal.

Tips For Success

Take advantage of the longer rest periods by picking weights that truly challenge you in the set rep ranges.

[Hybrid Pilates And Strength](https://saltbbs.com/ggx1y1hq/)

If you finish a set feeling like you had five reps left in the tank, go heavier, Martinez says.

Keep multiple sets of dumbbells handy—heavier for big lifts, lighter for accessory work.

Many blocks repeat twice, so push harder in the second round to maximize gains.

Sync your breath with your movement, exhaling and inhaling with each rep.

Don’t hesitate to modify. Moves like the assisted one-arm pushup are tough, and your coaches always demo modifications to keep you progressing.
