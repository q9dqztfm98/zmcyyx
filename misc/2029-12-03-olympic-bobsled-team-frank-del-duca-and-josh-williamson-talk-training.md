# Olympic Bobsled Team Frank Del Duca and Josh Williamson Talk Training, Tech, and Teamwork

The athletes of the 2026 Milano-Cortina Winter Games will give their all to go for gold on the snow and ice—but first, they'll have a chance to share everything you want to know about their sports while they're on the road. This is the Men's Health Muscle Car.

TEAM USA BOBSLEDDERS Frank Del Duca and Josh Williamson joined MH fitness director Ebenezer Samuel in the MH Muscle Car at the Milano-Cortina Games—and the athletes (Del Duca, especially) were ready to push Samuel to get them into some off-track competition even before their own event was underway.

"Look, an [Audi] RS 6 right by the Maserati," Del Duca said from his shotgun seat in the Muscle Car, a Maserati Grantourismo. "This is just... I'm going to get you into a race."

If Del Duca's teasing didn't make it clear, he's a car guy. But that's not all—he's also an Army Sergeant and member of the Army World Class Athlete Program (WCAP) and was one of two Team USA flag bearers at the Milano-Cortina Games's Opening Ceremony. In teaming with Williamson—a Floridian who got into bobsled as part of Team USA's Scouting Camp: Next Olympic Hopeful competition series—the pair is in prime position for a strong finish in the two-man bobsleigh field.

The keys to success? "Start fast, don't make mistakes, know the track, know the line, execute the line," Del Duca said. "Start fast, load well, drive well, work in the garage well, find speed in your equipment," added Williamson.

FDD: Yeah, Germany. They're very good at having really, really, good tech in their equipment without you really seeing it.

FDD: I sit up front, so for me to do core. Pallof press. Good [for] stability.

JW: Back squat. I think strength can be systemic throughout the whole body, and the back squat checks about every box. So I think that at the end of the day, if you can be a strong human, you can handle Gs better.

FDD: It depends on the lift, how about that?

MH: Do you have a pet peeve about your partner?

JW: Not really. So we spend a lot of time together. Frank is a lot more thorough than I am. I think I can get in a rush, and sometimes I get annoyed if we're doing the extra little thing—like Frank has collected the stickers on the sled so then we have them afterwards. For me (my wife hates this about me), I'm a throw it away kind of guy. Yeah, Frank does a much better job to make sure we keep the stuff, because when we ultimately need it and I've thrown it away, then it turns into a problem.

FDD: One of the differences in our approaches is more so, I can't really relax if we have stuff to do with bobsled, and it doesn't have to be done until tomorrow, but we're gonna do it today—I can't really relax till we get it done. So we've gone out for coffee, and I can't wait to go out for coffee with the guys and relax. But like, for me, it's like 20 minutes. We got the coffee, we had some good conversation. But now, we should do this and this and this.

JW: The coffee thing too—I'm a two to three hours in the morning, just sit at the coffee shop. That's one of my favorite things. If we were out of season. I would do the same thing with a beer.

FDD: And I can't sit still for two, three hours.

JW: But I would love to just have some conversation over a hot drink somewhere.

MH: Whose job is harder in bobsled?

JW: Inherently Frank's job, definitely.

FDD: A little more responsibility, and there's an additional level of preparation, because we're doing track walks and studying lines and trying new things. And so, yeah, there's a little bit more responsibility.

MH: What's the one muscle you think about when you think bobsled?

FDD: Would it be glutes?

JW: Low back. With the Gs, between the sliding and then the carrying the bobsled around your low back is something that you start to notice a lot more.

MH: What are the exercises that you guys hate to do?

FDD: Track walks from the bottom up.

MH: Do you guys have a hype song?

FDD: Linkin Park is stuff that I always used to listen to.

MH: Strength or cardio, which is more important?

FDD & JW: Strength.

MH: Fiber or protein?

FDD & JW: Protein.

MH: Best protein you found in the Olympic Village?

FDD: They have grilled chicken.

JW: Parmesan. Fresh parm in general, cheese—if you've actually seen on social media. There's been a big push, and I agree with this: It is a higher protein content for 100 grams than meat, chicken, beef. It's 36 grams of protein per 100 grams of parm. It's basically perfect.

MH: What non-sport could you win an Olympic medal in?

JW: If there was a sport for how long you can sit at a coffee shop and drink cortadas, I would probably be a gold medalist.

FDD: Working on things. I will start working on something when it's a non-training day, it's the offseason, and I'm just doing my hobbies, it's like a time warp. I don't know how long I could go without eating, without thinking of food, water, nothing, just working on something.

MH: Speaking of eating: Is it ever okay to eat in the car?

FDD: Not this one.

MH: Can You eat in a bobsled?

FDD: That would be a challenge trying to eat anything at all.

MH: Isn't that high-risk? Wouldn't you throw up?

FDD: I would take throwing up, though, over some of the physical dangers of sitting completely up and going into a corner and the cowling coming up and hitting you or potentially crashing.

MH: Does anyone even at the Olympic level throw up?

JW: One of our teammates, Emily Renna, always used to take dramamine while she was sliding, especially when she first started. And there's no shame in that, because this sport is very rough. The whole way down, instead of just thinking of twisting and turning, it's very rattly. People don't realize that when you're in the sled, you're shaking the whole way down. So even just as micro vibrations, you're doing that for a minute straight, a couple times a day.

FDD: You're gonna go lay down in bed and just be done for the day.

MH: So you met Snoop Dogg, right? What was that like?

FDD: It was cool. While meeting him, I was thinking I used to play Need for Speed Underground 2. You were on my parents TV because I was playing Need for Speed Underground 2, because he had a Riders on the Storm remix, and now he's on my parents TV and I'm on my parents TV with Snoop Dogg.

MH: If you compete with anybody in any sport, dead or alive, who would it be?

JW: [Tampa Bay Buccaneers fullback] Mike Alstott. I wouldn't want to play football with him, because he'd probably kill me. I think it'd be cool to get that guy in a bobsled.

FDD: I would like to get racers and drivers from other sports to come out and do bobsled.

MH: What would be the hardest thing for them to get used to in a bobsled?

FDD: Probably the sudden lateral movements and the neck. Formula One, they have super strong necks.

MH: What happened in Cool Runnings that people think is real, but is not?

FDD: We don't lean in the sled at all. For me, that's the big one.

MH: What's one thing they got right?

FDD: They really did get track and field athletes. They qualified for the games and started the Jamaican Bobsled Federation.

MH: How does weight work in your sport?

FDD: The sleds in each discipline have a minimum weight. And then the sled and crew, and any sort of equipment that goes down, say, the covers for your shoes, if you run with those—that has a maximum limit. We'll be weighed after the first run of the competition, and then if you're overweight, you're done. Disqualified. And then if your sled is underweight, you're done.

JW: On top of that, how we're training—I want to be as fast as I can, but I'm also trying to be as heavy as I can be. Kind of vice versa, I want to get a lot faster, but I can't just lose a bunch of weight. So you're kind of training like a sprinter while also training like an Olympic weightlifter.
