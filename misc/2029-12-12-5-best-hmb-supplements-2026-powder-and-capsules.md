What Does HMB Stand For?

HMB, also known as Beta-hydroxy-beta-methylbutyrate, is produced naturally in the body due to an amino acid breakdown [1].

Among various amino acids that perform different functions inside our body, leucine helps stimulate protein synthesis and inhibit protein breakdown, especially in muscles.

When our body breaks down this branched-chain amino acid, leucine, HMB is produced.

However, only 5% of leucine is broken down to produce HMB [2], due to which supplements are often taken to avail its maximum benefits.

While not enough human trials have been carried out to assess the actual long-term effects of HMB, current research suggests that it has prospective benefits.

One study suggested a positive correlation between HMB and muscle growth, provided that individuals also performed resistance training [3].

Another review involving 7 different studies suggested a muscle gain in individuals of approximately 0.78 pounds after using HMB for more than 2 months [4].

On the other hand, few major studies done on HMB showed no muscle growth due to HMB consumption. There were no changes in body composition parameters, and overall strength remained unaffected even after taking the HMB dosage for considerable days [5].

One study [6] involving patients with certain conditions, like AIDS and cancer, showed that these affected individuals were able to retain muscle despite their health condition. This means that HMB has the potential to help individuals improve lean muscle mass.

The results from another study [7] suggest that HMB may promote fat loss and enhance athletic abilities. Subjects that used the HMB supplement generated more power in high-intensity workouts.

Again, there is still insufficient data, but HMB has shown in several studies that it is able to improve the recovery process and effectively heal wounds. It’s also been shown to reduce the expression of inflammatory markers [8] such as TNF-α and IL-8.

Participants taking HMB were also tested to have lower levels of LDL or bad cholesterol. Systolic blood pressures also seemed to drop due to HMB intake.

HMB Supplement Side Effects

Since HMB is a naturally occurring compound, it doesn’t show any severe side effects in the body.

Occasional side effects you may experience include:

Constipation

Some users may experience constipation when taking HMB as their body adjusts to processing the supplement.

To reduce this risk, prioritize a fiber-rich diet and stay properly hydrated. Both play a key role in preventing constipation.

If constipation persists, consult a healthcare provider. They can determine whether the supplement or another factor is the cause.

Itching

Itching can often result as the body’s reaction to certain chemicals or compounds, and in the case of HMB, it might be due to a mild allergic reaction or the body’s temporary response to the new substance.

If itching becomes consistent after taking HMB, it’s essential to discontinue use and seek advice from a healthcare professional.

Keeping the skin moisturized and avoiding scratching can help alleviate the symptom, but pinpointing the root cause is pivotal.

Rashness

When introduced to new dietary supplements like HMB, the body might develop rashes as a reaction to unfamiliar compounds or as a sign of a mild allergic response.

If a rash appears after taking HMB, it’s important to halt its consumption immediately and consult with a healthcare provider to ensure it’s not a more severe allergic reaction or another underlying issue.

Stomach ache

When taking supplements such as HMB, stomachaches might result from the body trying to digest and process the supplement, especially if consumed on an empty stomach or in large doses.

To mitigate this, it’s advisable to take HMB with food or split the doses.

If stomach pain persists or is severe, it’s crucial to seek medical attention and consider if HMB is suitable for one’s health regimen.
