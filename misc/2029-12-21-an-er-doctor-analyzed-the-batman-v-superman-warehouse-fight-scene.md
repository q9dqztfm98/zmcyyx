# An ER Doctor Analyzed the 'Batman v. Superman' Warehouse Fight Scene

Nobody goes to see a superhero movie for realism. Audiences want larger-than-life characters with completely made-up powers, engaging in epic battles where entire buildings get toppled and the main character is able to shrug off the kind of brutal violence that would kill a normal person several times over.

But it is fun to think about just how bad some of these fight scenes would be in real life, which is why Dr. Ed Hope, an emergency medicine specialist, makes videos in which he provides an accurate diagnosis of the injuries in fight sequences from superhero stories like Marvel's Captain America: The Winter Soldier and Amazon's hit The Boys. In his most recent video, Hope breaks down every bone-crunching, blood-spattering blow from the infamous warehouse fight in DC's smackdown movie Batman v. Superman: Dawn of Justice.

The scene starts with a bang—quite literally—as Ben Affleck's Batman explodes out of the floor of the warehouse, throwing his opponents in all directions. "Any injuries like this that are caused by landing awkwardly will be called a tertiary blast injury, and most concerning will be any injury to the head or neck," Hope explains.

The scene also includes multiple bone breakages and dislocations which, while not fatal, would be extremely painful and justify the anguished yelling that can be heard from Batman's enemies. More serious, however, is the penetrating chest trauma, a.k.a. when Batman fully just stabs a guy in the chest. "We'd have to rule out a pneumothorax, haemothorax, these injuries have a mortality rate of 50 percent," he says. "This knife has also been in Batman, so you'd have to think of blood-borne virus transmission."

Seeing as how triage is his area of expertise, Hope concludes the video by assessing the condition of each of Batman's opponents at the end of the showdown, in ascending order of seriousness.

"My best guess would be two stable, four in serious condition, six in critical condition, and four dead at the scene," he says.
