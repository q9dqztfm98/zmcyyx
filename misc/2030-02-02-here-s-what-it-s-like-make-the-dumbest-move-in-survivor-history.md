# Here's What it's Like Make the 'Dumbest' Move in 'Survivor' History

• On May 8, 2008, one of the greatest power moves in Survivor history aired in TV.• Cirie, Amanda, Parvati, and Natalie convinced Erik to give up his immunity necklace.• Here's how the legendary Survivor moment went down.

Today is the 11th anniversary of one of the biggest moves (if not THE biggest move) in Survivor history: the blindside of Erik Reichenbach. Most fans of the long-running CBS series know the story of the Survivor: Micronesia—Fans vs. Favorites episode that aired on May 8, 2008, but in case you need a refresher, Cirie, Natalie, Parvati, and Amanda were able to convince challenge winner Erik to give his immunity necklace to Natalie—and then blindside him.

In a delightful oral history over at Entertainment Weekly, the castaways involved in the legendary blindside explained their side of the story.

Why did Erik give up immunity?

At the time, Erik was a 21-year-old from a small town in Michigan with a desire to be liked by those around him. He was also surrounded by a bunch of skilled returning players. In order to get into his psyche, the "Black Widow Brigade" preyed on his emotions in the hours before Tribal Council.

After Erik won his second immunity challenge in a row, Cirie mused about whether Erik would give the necklace to Natalie, who he had previously been allied with. She said her throwaway question was a "spark" that led to a "bonfire," and the ladies started to formulate a plan to get Erik to give up the necklace.

The four women told Erik that he was untrustworthy and everyone was mad at him, and that he needed to redeem himself in the game and in the eyes of the jury. They told him that the only way for him to do so would be for him to give up immunity to Natalie. Erik, Cirie, and Natalie were then supposed to vote for Amanda or Parvati to go home. Erik explained to EW how they used his emotions against him:

"Really, what they did was change it from a strategic conversation to an emotional conversation. That was really one of the big turning points in that day is that it went from me thinking about strategic placement of myself in a game versus social placement within a structure of people. They put me in a very bad emotional place."

How did the plan come into action?

As the five castaways headed to Tribal Council, Erik had warmed up to the idea of giving his immunity necklace to Natalie, but nothing was set in stone yet. Parvati, the eventual winner of the season, explained that each of the women had a role to play once they got to Tribal. She told EW:

"My role was to be the bully. I took on that role because that’s already what Erik expected of me. He didn’t like me very much. And I thought, 'You know, he thinks I’ve already betrayed a bunch of people, so he’s going to anticipate me being a bad guy. And I’m going to do that. I’m going to just bully him and then see how that works.' Amanda was good cop, I was bad cop, Natalie was the one who was the underdog who needed Erik’s help, and then Cirie was this motherly, nurturing, you-can-trust-me figure."

The group ganged up on Erik during the tribal, which was brutal to watch, but necessary for the plan to work. Natalie said that she was "kind of shocked" at how the Tribal Council went. Ozzy, who was on the jury, said the mental and physical toll of the game may have made an impact on Erik's decision, and the ladies "did a good job" of taking advantage of that.

As we all now know, Erik eventually gave up his immunity necklace, and he was subsequently voted out.

How do Cirie, Amanda, Parvati, and Natalie feel about the blindside now?

The ladies agreed that while the move was needed to keep them in the game, they do feel a little bad about how it all went down. Amanda even told EW she "still feels bad about it to this day. I don’t think I ever told him that, either."

How does Erik feel about the blindside now?

Erik said fans were kind to him, even after his bad decision. However, his father took his elimination pretty hard—he apparently "punched a punching bag for two hours straight." Erik went on to say that once the Survivor community rallied around him, his father grew to understand why he did what he did.

Ultimately, Erik told EW he wouldn't have done anything differently:

"I’m not going to stop trusting people. I’m not going to stop believing that people have good intentions. You have to be flexible.
