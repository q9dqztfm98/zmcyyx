What hematocrit measures

Hematocrit reflects the proportion of your blood taken up by red blood cells, the cells that carry oxygen from your lungs to the rest of your body. It is usually reported alongside hemoglobin and red blood cell count as part of a complete blood count. Because it is a ratio, hematocrit rises when red cells increase or when plasma volume drops.

What high or low levels mean

A low hematocrit often points to anemia and can result from blood loss, iron, vitamin B12, or folate deficiencies, chronic disease, or reduced red cell production. Symptoms may include fatigue, weakness, or shortness of breath.

A high hematocrit most commonly reflects dehydration, which concentrates red cells by lowering plasma volume. It can also occur with chronic low-oxygen states such as lung disease, sleep apnea, or living at high altitude, or with polycythemia, a condition of overproduced red cells. Because hydration shifts the value, a single reading can be misleading. Follow up with your provider if your result sits outside the reference range, especially if it is paired with symptoms or an abnormal hemoglobin.

Hematocrit FAQ

What is the difference between hematocrit and hemoglobin?

Hemoglobin measures the oxygen-carrying protein inside red cells, while hematocrit measures the percentage of blood volume those cells occupy. They usually move together, and hematocrit runs roughly three times the hemoglobin value.

Can dehydration raise your hematocrit?

Yes. When you are dehydrated, plasma volume falls and red cells become more concentrated, pushing hematocrit higher. Rehydrating often returns the value to normal, so timing and fluid status can affect your result.

Is a slightly high hematocrit dangerous?

A mildly elevated hematocrit is often benign and linked to dehydration or lifestyle factors. Persistently high levels warrant evaluation, as thicker blood can raise clotting risk and may signal an underlying condition.

Read alongside hemoglobin, red blood cell count, red cell distribution width, and the complete blood count.
