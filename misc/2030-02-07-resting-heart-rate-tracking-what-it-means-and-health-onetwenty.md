# Resting Heart Rate: Tracking What It Means and Health | OneTwenty

Resting heart rate is how many times your heart beats per minute when your body is fully at rest, and most wearables estimate it during sleep or long periods of stillness. Within the healthy range, a lower resting heart rate generally reflects a fitter, more efficient heart. What your device shows day to day matters less than the direction your baseline moves over weeks.

What resting heart rate measures

Your resting heart rate captures how hard your cardiovascular system works to meet your body's baseline oxygen demand. A wearable reads it optically at the wrist or finger, usually pulling from your calmest stretches such as deep sleep. Because a stronger heart pumps more blood with each beat, it needs fewer beats to do the same job. That is why resting heart rate is treated as a quick, high-signal readout of overall conditioning and recovery rather than a diagnosis on its own.

A lower resting heart rate within the healthy range is one of the more reliable everyday signs of good cardiovascular fitness. As aerobic fitness improves, stroke volume rises and resting heart rate tends to settle downward, which is why endurance athletes often sit well below the general adult range. Large population studies have also linked a persistently elevated resting heart rate with higher long-term cardiovascular and mortality risk, even in people without diagnosed disease. Just as important, a rising trend in your own baseline can flag poor sleep, dehydration, stress, illness, or overtraining before you feel it. That early-warning quality is what makes the metric worth watching over time.

How to improve your resting heart rate

Regular aerobic training is the strongest lever, since consistent cardio gradually lowers resting heart rate as fitness builds. Prioritizing sleep, managing stress, staying well hydrated, and limiting alcohol all help your baseline settle. Give your body real recovery between hard sessions, because overreaching can push resting heart rate up. Track a 7 to 14 day trend instead of reacting to one high morning. This is general education, not medical advice.

Resting heart rate FAQ

What is a good resting heart rate?

For most adults, 60 to 100 bpm is typical, and many fit people sit in the 50s or lower. Your personal baseline is the better reference point.

Can a resting heart rate be too low?

A low resting heart rate is often a sign of fitness, but if it comes with dizziness, fainting, or fatigue, have it checked by a clinician.

Why does my resting heart rate change day to day?

Sleep, hydration, alcohol, caffeine, stress, and early illness all nudge it. Short-term swings are normal, so watch the trend.

Read alongside Heart Rate Variability (HRV), Blood Pressure, and VO₂ max.
