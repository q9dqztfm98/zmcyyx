Design and Features

The T1 Pro is the best everyday razor of the two, and answers the question, what if my jade roller could shave? As a grooming editor, I’ve tested a lot of electric razors, and I can confidently say this is the slimmest I’ve tried. It features a T-shape similar to the Panasonic Arc5 at roughly half the thickness and a fourth of the weight—albeit with four fewer razors. Still, the motor delivers 12,000 cuts per minute, which is remarkable given its slenderness. For comparison, that’s the same motor speed as the Philips Noreclo OneBlade. This isn’t too surprising, given that the T1 Pro is the first electric razor I’ve found that can match the OneBlade’s lightness and compactness.

The T1 Pro is armed with stainless steel, waterproof blades for a smooth shave and dual shock absorption for a steady, comfortable grip. The body is made of aerospace-grade aluminum and is, without a doubt, the slimmest electric razor I’ve tested. It also comes with three trimmer guards for managing short beards and scruff, and a blade cover.

The P3 Pro shares a similar design to the best electric razor for travel, the Panasonic Palm-Sized Arc 5. Just like the 2025 Grooming Award winner, it features a compact, rectangular design that’s small enough for the most snug Dopp Kits. While it has a bit more heft to it, the P3 Pro also packs foil blades and a 12,000-cuts-per-minute motor, like the T1 Pro. While the P3 Pro doesn’t include any trimmer guards, it does have a blade cover. Additionally, both devices are chargeable with a basic USB-C, which is super convenient because it’s one less cord I need to store or travel with.

Effectiveness

Compared to the OneBlade, which relies on trimmer-style blades, the T1 Pro gives a closer shave with its 12,000 cuts per minute. However, the flip side of that is you have to move much slower with the T1 or you risk tugging and cuts. Still, if you take it slow, this razor is gentle and precise, getting as close a shave as you’ll get without going over $150 on the device. Not too many electric razors include shave guards for maintaining stubble, so as someone who often likes to leave a little scruff, I appreciated the inclusion. The three sizes successfully turn the T1 Pro into an effective short-length beard trimmer when on.

Where the T1 performs better than any other electric razor I’ve used—including the unmatched Braun Series 9 Pro+—is shaving around the lips and right under the nose. This spot always causes me issues with other electric razors, as I need to go over it multiple times, stretch out my skin, and make insane faces to allow the razors to properly tackle it. But with the T1, once over was enough, thanks to its slim razor head—and I didn’t have to contort my face in any way. The P3 Pro is the ideal travel razor of the two; however, the T1 Pro won’t weigh you down like other larger daily driver razors if you choose to travel with it.

Its slim design doesn’t take up much room in a bag, and there’s even a travel lock to prevent it from going off inside. In fact, the T1 Pro is actually lighter, nearly 100 grams so, than the P3 Pro. Plus, it has a solid battery life: 120 minutes off a full charge, which only takes 15 minutes. My biggest nitpick with the T1 is that it can clog up pretty quickly; however, the bladehead releases magnetically, making emptying the hair super easy. I do this two or three times during a shave, and it keeps shaving perfectly.

If you’re tight on travel space, the P3 Pro is perfect. The compact design fits in the smallest toiletry bags, and shaves just as close as the T1 Pro. That being said, it is heavier, so keep that in mind if you’re managing the weight of your luggage. The battery life is just under the P3 Pro, 100 minutes off 40 minutes of charge. But the blades cut just as close, and feel equally gentle on skin.

How to Clean

Both Laifen razors are IPX7 waterproof, meaning you can drop the device in water up to 1 meter deep for 30 minutes, and it won’t be permanently damaged. With that in mind, I simply stand over a garbage can and take off the magnetic head to let the hair that’s built up fall out. Then, I run the device’s blade under the faucet for a few seconds. After a month of use, they each still look brand new.

Value

The T1 Pro is about $100 more expensive than its closest design relative, the Philips Norelco OneBlade, but it shaves much closer while still offering the option of managing stubble that has made the OneBlade such a great tool. If you like the slim style of that device and prefer to stay cleanly shaved—or change your facial hair often and want more options—the T1 Pro is well worth the extra Benjamin. The P3 Pro, on the other hand, is over $100 cheaper than the similarly designed Panasonic Palm-Sized Arc5. To be clear, the Arc5 provides a closer shave and a more powerful motor, but $300 is a lot for a dedicated travel tool. At $180, the P3 is a better budget option with only a slight drop off in efficiency.

Final Verdict: Should You Buy the New Laifen Razors?

If you want sleek, powerful grooming tools that feel more Apple Store than beauty aisle, Laifen’s T1 Pro and P3 Pro razors deliver. The T1 Pro is the slimmest electric shaver I've tested—lighter than the OneBlade, with 12,000 cuts per minute, dual shock absorption, and a surgical-sharp shave that handles tight spots (like under the nose) better than even the Braun Series 9 Pro+. It includes three stubble guards, charges fast via USB-C, and lasts 120 minutes.

For travel, the P3 Pro punches above its weight with the same motor power, foil blades, and a compact, palm-sized body that fits any Dopp kit. Both razors are waterproof, easy to clean, and look as sharp as they shave. While the T1 Pro costs more than its closest competitor, it delivers added precision and versatility. The P3 Pro, meanwhile, is a more affordable alternative to luxury travel razors, like the Palm-Sized Arc5. Clean, portable, and built like tech—these are sleek grooming game-changers.
