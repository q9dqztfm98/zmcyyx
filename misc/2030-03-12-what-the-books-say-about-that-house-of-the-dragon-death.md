How does Rhaenys die in George R. R. Martin's Fire & Blood book?

Things play out a bit differently in Fire & Blood, George R. R. Martin's textbook-esque source material for House of the Dragon. In that book, it's only confirmed that Meleys died. But next to Meleys's body, another body that was so burnt and blackened—and was widely believed to be Rhaenys's—was found.

Not quite the same closure as in the show (and the show has played around with this kind of Fire & Blood uncertainty before), but it's about as much evidence as possible just short of being entirely certain.

We'll see how House of the Dragon plays things out from here. But in Fire & Blood, Rhaenys's death at Rook's Rest creates a rift between Rhaenyra and Lord Corlys, who is angered and heartbroken by his wife's death, believing that Rhaenyra should have either gone herself or sent one of her sons.
