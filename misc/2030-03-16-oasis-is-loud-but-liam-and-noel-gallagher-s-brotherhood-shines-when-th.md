# Oasis Is Loud. But Liam and Noel Gallagher's Brotherhood Shines When They're Most Quiet.

AS RECENTLY AS a year ago, an image of Liam and Noel Gallagher walking on stage, hand in hand as the hard rockin' instrumental opening of “Fuckin in the Bushes” plays, could’ve been written off as some kind of far-off fantasy or delirious mirage. But there it was, not long after nightfall overtook a late summer red-tinted northern New Jersey sunset, on the Sunday of Labor Day weekend at MetLife Stadium. The Gallagher brothers took the stage in a space that soon will be occupied by the New York Jets and Giants, but this band, centered on a pair of oft-quarreling siblings in their 50s, are without question a bigger draw than even a pair of the NFL’s marquee teams—combined. And a key reason for that is the sheer unlikelihood that we’d ever arrive at this moment; These aren’t just brothers who occasionally didn’t get along—this is a duo who hated each other, dragging names through the mud for more than a decade after their 2009 breakup.

Liam, the frontman, and Noel, the songwriter and lead guitarist, first rose to fame with the iconic British rock group in the '90s, creators of undeniable hits like “Wonderwall” and “Don’t Look Back in Anger." Throughout the years, though, the Gallagher name transformed from one that at first—with the release of formative albums Definitely Maybe and What's The Story (Morning Glory)—symbolized old school rock 'n' roll magic, but eventually become synonymous with relentless bickering and seemingly irreparable feuding (a reality such a shorthand that an Oscar-winning film director even joked about it with me earlier this year). And yet, despite all of that and with the benefit of time, here we were.

But the big, bold gestures of formerly feuding brothers hugging and holding hands—a sign to the sold-out MetLife crowd of 70,000 that any issues, for now, have ceased—weren’t what most caught my eye. What I kept noticing during Oasis’s nearly two-hour set were the “quieter” moments (as quiet as a moment in a football stadium filled with die-hard fans straining their voices to reach the highest of high notes, at least)—Liam singing with his chest puffed out, sunglasses on his face at night and wearing a parka in the midst of summer, with his arms folded behind his back, while Noel picked away at his guitar just a few paces away. Some might even call what they were doing nothing. But there's a naturalness in that nothing—the unspoken, unmentioned comfort between a pair of performers who are meant to create things and share a stage together. It was the repeated image of this moment, one song after another, that sold me on the fact that these brothers really were back more than any handshake or hug.

There’s history between Liam and Noel—obviously, in more ways than this piece could even scratch the surface of—but for the purposes of a live show, you can tell when a band has been playing together for years, and when they've just put the pieces together for the first time. As someone with a sibling myself (although not as someone who plays music) I can say from experience that there's a shorthand that comes without any effort. Even if it's just hanging out or having a conversation, it's a natural energy between siblings that picks up like it's nothing, effortlessly, whether it’s been days, weeks, or months since the last time. There's no curve in getting back to the way things have always been.

The late-summer show at MetLife wasn’t my first time seeing Oasis on their reunion tour, but it was my first time actually seeing Oasis. Back in July, I took a trip with some college friends to Europe—centered on attending the band's first show back in their hometown of Manchester. It was my first time in Europe, and my experiences in Dublin and London (bookending the Manchester portion of our trip) won’t be forgotten. But it was the show at Manchester’s Heaton Park that was the main attraction, and as Liam Gallagher would say, it was “Biblical.” I’d never experienced energy like that; I’d never heard audiences singing along like that; I’d never seen a show like that. I also literally didn’t see the show, because from my 5’8’’ vantage point (and with thousands of people jumping and having a blast in front of me), I had to rely heavily on video displays, vibes, and just singing along. It didn’t matter—it was one of the best things I’d ever experienced. But actually seeing the show at MetLife gave a whole different perspective.

The show is structured in a way that gives both Gallaghers their moment—and gives both the opportunity to bask in love for (and from) the hyped-up crowd. Part of the historic rift within the band was always that one brother would feel they weren’t quite on equal footing with the other; Liam’s big personality is always going to shine, and it does during a show even when he’s not crooning lyrics like its still 1996. You'll find him throughout the set shaking maracas, dedicating "Live Forever" to the victims of a recent terrible tragedy, or bantering about whether he’s in New York or New Jersey or “Wherever the fuck we are.” They simply do not make rock stars like that in a factory on a conveyor belt; He is one of one.

Noel, meanwhile, takes a mid-show run of the songs he leads, both giving his brother’s vocal chords a rest and giving him a little moment at the center of things. “Little by Little” isn’t from one of the band’s most cherished albums, but its chorus is an unquestioned singalong, one even newer casual fans could pick up on and have a great time with. "Talk Tonight" makes for a magical moment on stage.

As performers, the best way to describe Oasis is efficient. They have a setlist filled with hits, and work through them with energy and passion. Liam likes to say things, and give little mini-rants, but for the most part they keep things moving. People want to hear the songs, and from early-set sing-alongs like “Acquiesce” to the encore that includes “The Masterplan,” “Don’t Look Back in Anger,” and “Wonderwall,” each song has its own real, specific, unforgettable moment. I’ve always thought “Half The World Away” was one of the band’s best; On the original recording, you can hear Noel’s voice belting, straining, as he sings the lyrics about longing for something better. The years have only given the emotion in his voice more depth. By the time the band closes the show out with “Champagne Supernova” and accompanying fireworks, there’s not much more you could’ve asked for (My personal favorite deep cut is “Stay Young,” but we can’t get everything, can we?)

[Liam and Noel](https://980187.com/a6aawq3pg/)

Maybe we can’t get everything. But if Oasis can come back after 15 years away—and if Liam and Noel Gallagher can put their issues aside not only for the flashy moments that make headlines, but the quiet, real-life familial moments too—then it’s hard to really rule anything out. An Oasis show (and, really, any big show that someone would get into and get excited for) is going to be a big, memorable moment. But when you know the backstory, and understand what the songs mean not only to the audience but to the people playing them, it can cut deep. It can be inspirational.

It can even be aspirational—If Liam and Noel can make it work, there’s no reason the rest of us can’t do the same
