# 40 Actors Who Turned Down Iconic Movie Roles

Your favorite movies were almost very, very different. As a general rule, the actors who end up appearing in films are often not the first choice for roles. This means iconic movies could have been extremely different if ideal casting panned out. Just how different? Let’s just say this alternate silver screen universe is hard to even imagine.

Find out which actor was slated to play Forrest Gump before Tom Hanks cinche the role, and which actor almost stole the role of Buddy the Elf from Will Farrell. You’ll never look at your favorite films the same way again.

Jack Nicholson as Michael Corleone in The Godfather

Jack Nicholson turned down the iconic role as The Godfather in the 1972 film. Nicholson reportedly told Movieline in 2004 that he thought the role should have been played by an Italian.

John Travolta as Forrest Gump in Forrest Gump

Forrest Gump wouldn’t be the same without Tom Hanks at the helm of the film. But Hanks wasn’t the first choice for the film’s title character. John Travolta was offered the role, but turned it down to make Pulp Fiction.

Will Smith as Neo in The Matrix

[Iconic Movie](https://saltbbs.com/7dh9xd6r/)

Before Keanu Reeves cinched the role of Neo in The Matrix, Will Smith was considered for the role. On his YouTube channel, Smith explained he turned down the role—but he is not proud of the decision. He said the pitch for the movie seemed confusing and lackluster. Smith made Wild Wild West instead.

Robin Williams as Jack Torrance in The Shining

Robin Williams was almost cast as axe-murderer Jack Torrance in The Shining. Though it’s hard to imagine anyone but Jack Nicholson in the role, Williams was reportedly director Stanely Kubrick’s top choice.

Leonardo DiCaprio in Brokeback Mountain

Before Heath Ledger and Jake Gyllenhal met on Brokeback Mountain, Leonardo DiCaprio was approached for a role in the film and turned it down. Leo wasn’t the only one to pass on the film. Brad Pitt and Matt Damon also said no to proposed roles.

John Lithgow as The Joker in Batman

When Tim Burton was brought on to direct Batman in the late 1980s, his team approached Lithgow to bring The Joker to life. At the pair’s first meeting, Lithgow ended up talking Burton out of even considering him for the role, telling Conan O’Brien in 2019 that he thought at the time, “Who is going to want to see a movie about Batman?”

Burt Renyolds as James Bond in Live and Let Die

Burt Renyolds turned down the opportunity to play Bond in 1973’s Live and Let Die, telling USA Today in 2015 that he rejected the part because he thought the public wouldn't accept an American 007.

Christina Applegate as Elle Woods in Legally Blonde

It’s hard to imagine anyone but Reese Witherspoon playing sorority girl-turned-lawyer Elle Woods. Christina Applegate was offered the role of Woods before Witherspoon, turning it down for a specific reason.

"I got scared of kind of repeating myself," Applegate told Entertainment Tonight in 2015. "What a stupid move that was, right?"

Sean Connery as Gandalf in the Lord of the Rings franchise

Producers for the Lord of the Rings franchise reportedly offered Sean Connery $10 million per film and 15% of box office earnings to play Gandalf. Connery turned down the role, saying he “did not understand the script.” As a result, he reportedly missed out on a $450 million dollar payday.

Al Pacino as Han Solo in the Star Wars series

Al Pacino was almost part of the Star Wars universe as a top choice to play Han Solo. But the actor said he didn’t understand the script at all, turning down the film.

"It was at that time in my career where I was offered everything," Pacino told MTV in 2014. "I was in 'The Godfather.' They didn't care if I was right or wrong for the role, if I could act or not act."

Matt Damon as Harvey Dent in The Dark Knight

Matt Damon turned down the role of Two-Face in The Dark Knight, freeing up the role for Aaron Eckhart. Damon told MTV that the decision simply came down to scheduling.

"Look, Aaron is a great actor, so the movie didn't suffer for it," said Damon. "Every now and then you get one and you can't do it."

Harrison Ford as Alan Grant in Jurassic Park

Harrison Ford almost ran amongst the dinosaurs in Jurassic Park. The actor reportedly turned down the role of Alan Grant in the film, though the exact reason why is undisclosed.

Johnny Depp as Ferris Bueller in Ferris Bueller’s Day Off

Johnny Depp is widely reported to have turned down the title role in Ferris Bueller’s Day Off, though the exact reason why remains a mystery.

Emily Blunt as Black Window in Iron Man 2

Emily Blunt turned down the chance to play Black Widow, telling Vulture in 2014 that the decision came down to scheduling. In fact, Scarlett Johansson was originally given a rejection call in favor of Blunt. It was only when Blunt turned the role down that Johansson booked the film.

Tom Selleck as Indian Jones in Raiders of the Lost Ark

Tom Selleck was offered the role of Indiana Jones, but reportedly couldn’t free up time for the role given his Magnum P.I. contract. Instead, Harrison Ford took on the iconic role.

Marilyn Monroe as Holly Golightly in Breakfast at Tiffany’s

Author Truman Capote, who wrote the novella Breakfast at Tiffany’s, was open in his desire for Marilyn Monroe to play Holly Golightly in the film adaptation. The exact reason Monroe didn’t star in the film is unknown, though Capote is claimed to not have liked Audrey Hepburn’s portrayal of Golightly.

Nicolas Cage as Aragorn in Lord of the Rings

Nicolas Cage turned down the chance to star in Lord of the Rings due to a busy schedule. Though he told Newsweek he doesn’t regret the move, but he does wish circumstances would have allowed him to take the role.

Ian McKellen as Dumbledore in the Harry Potter series

When Richard Harris died in 2002, Ian McKellen was approached to take on the role of Dumbledore in the Harry Potter series. McKellen turned down the role, telling the BBC in 2017 he couldn’t “take over the part from an actor who I’d known didn’t approve of me.” Harris once was quote saying that McKellen was “technically brilliant, but passionless.”

Molly Ringwald as Vivian Ward in Pretty Woman

In a 2012 Reddit AMA, Molly Ringwald revealed she had once been approached for the lead in Pretty Woman and reviewed the initial script. Ringwald didn’t share why she didn’t pursue the role.

Henry Winkler as Danny Zuko in Grease

Henry Winkler aka Fonzie turned down the role of Danny Zuko in Grease, telling AARP in 2017 that he “didn’t want to be typecast.” While Winkler said he didn’t regret the decision, he hinted he would take the role if he could do it over.

“It’s not now that I’m, like, ‘Oh, I should’ve done the role,’” Winkler told the magazine. “It’s that I turned it down intellectually instead of instinctually. Listen to your gut—it knows everything.”
