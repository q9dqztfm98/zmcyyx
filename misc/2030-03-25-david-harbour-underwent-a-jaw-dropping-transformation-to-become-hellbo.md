# David Harbour Underwent a Jaw-Dropping Transformation to Become Hellboy

You probably know actor David Harbour for his supporting roles, namely that of officer Jim Hopper from Stranger Things. But Harbour's newest role is anything but that of a supporting actor— he's slated to play the next Hellboy, which is currently rumored to be released sometime in 2019. To the delight of Hellboy fans, the movie decided to release the first images showing off Harbour in all his Hellboy glory. All we can say is damn, this new Hellboy is jacked.

We've seen our fair share of ripped superhero bodies, and Hellboy is up there as one of the most impressive. Which makes sense, seeing as the comics describe Hellboy as having three qualities— red skin, two horns and the body of an MMA fighter. There's only so much Hollywood magic can do.

[Harbour Underwent Jaw-Dropping Transformation](https://therapy.pepgen.cc/compare/cosrx-ultimate-nourishing-rice-overnight-spa-mask-versus-the-ordinary-niacinamide-10-zinc-1-6aa73a5c8096/)

To prepare for what he described as the most physically demanding role in his life "by a long shot," Harbour enlisted the help of celebrity trainer Don Saladino, who trained Men's Health cover guy Ryan Reynolds for his role in Deadpool. "I wanted to mainly focus on power and strength so when I'm doing these movements and doing this role that you believe it, that this guy, when he hits you, hits you like a truck," he said. Based on the end result, we'd say he was successful in that.

Harbour said in an interview posted on Saladino's blog that prior to beginning training, "I was a chubby kid growing up. Straight out of college, I did my first play, which I got pretty in shape for, but I let that go completely. So from about the time I was 26 years old, to about now, I haven’t really been training at the gym seriously at all."

Saladino documented Harbour's transformation, which lasted a total of two months, and released a video on his blog detailing his training. Just as Harbour said, the video showed focus on building strength and power, particularly a lot of upper body focus utilizing farmer's carries (no wonder his shoulders are so huge), bench presses, rows and plenty of machine work.
