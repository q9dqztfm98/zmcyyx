Once you see Everything Everywhere All at Once and the initial shock wears off, you may start to wonder, "Are there any other movies like that?" All you'll want after is another movie with as much action, sci-fi, and genuine heart as the Oscar-nominated drama. Starring Michelle Yeoh, Stephanie Hsu, Ke Huy Quan, and Jamie Lee Curtis, the film follows Evelyn, a Chinese immigrant who runs a laundromat. When it's time to do her taxes, Evelyn takes her family with her, only to discover she's able to travel to other universes and the fate of various parallel universes rests on her shoulders. That's all happening simultaneously as she has a lot of major problems of her own happening in her own universe, too. There's far more jam-packed into the 2 hour and 19 minute runtime, but to say more would be to spoil an incredible film.

For a movie as wild as Everything Everywhere All at Once, you need to recommend films that are equally as mind-blowing. Plus, with the many genres the film touches on (drama, comedy, sci-fi, action, arguably even romance), there's a wide range of movies to choose from that are just as strange, but also can pull at the heartstrings. Whether you're looking for an emotional rollercoaster, a visual mind-bender, or just one of the weirdest films you've ever seen, trust us, we've got you covered.

Here are 26 movies we recommend if you loved Everything Everywhere All at Once as much as we did.

Antibirth (2016)

This body horror film stars Natasha Lyonne as Lou, who begins to experience symptoms of pregnancy when she wakes up after a blackout night of partying. As she goes about her day, Lou pieces together the previous night and has strange hallucinations that'll make you cringe.

Seven Psychopaths (2012)

Martin McDonagh (of Banshees of Inisherin fame) directs Seven Psychopaths, starring Colin Farrell, Woody Harrelson, Sam Rockwell, and Christopher Walken. The satirical meta thriller follows a writer struggling to come up with seven psychopaths for his screenplay. With the help of his friends, he manages to find all seven while going on a ridiculous adventure.

The Matrix (1999)

If you're looking for a mind-bending thriller like Everything Everywhere All at Once, the obvious answer is The Matrix. Starring Keanu Reeves, the sci-fi blockbuster follows Neo, a hacker who realizes he's living in a simulation and joins with a group of rebels to break humanity free of its trapped existence.

Being John Malkovich (1999)

For surreal comedy, look no further than Being John Malkovich. While working in an office, a man discovers a small door. Where does the door lead, you may ask? It leads to actor John Malkovich's brain, where the man discovers he can see the world through John Malkovich's eyes. When one of his coworkers discovers the door, chaos ensues.

Spider-Man: Into the Spider-Verse

The Marvel movie even non-Marvel fans will enjoy, Into the Spider-Verse follows Miles Morales in his own Spider-Man origin story. When he discovers a collider with access to parallel universes, Miles also finds out Spider-Men from other universes are now in his universe, and he has to not only take down the collider, but bring them back to their homes.

The Paper Tigers (2020)

This marital arts action comedy follows three former kung-fu prodigies who team up in their older age to avenge their master's death.

Colossal (2016)

Anne Hathaway stars in this dark comedy about an alcoholic comedy writer who realizes she can manifest and control a strange creature on the other side of the world. As she seeks to understand more about her condition, she faces hard truths about her life.

Scott Pilgrim Vs. The World (2010)

This popular action comedy romcom from director Edgar Wright stars Michael Cera and is based on a graphic novel series of the same name. When a bassist meets a delivery girl, he becomes determined to be with her. To date her, Scott realizes he has to defeat all of her evil exes first.
