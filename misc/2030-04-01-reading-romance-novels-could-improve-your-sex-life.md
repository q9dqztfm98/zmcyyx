GUYS, IT'S TIME to get in on the romance book hype. Gone are the days when romance books are solely created by and for women. With over 19 million romance paperback books sold in 2022 alone, the genre is booming—and with this growth comes new voices, new plots, new love interests (alien ice barbarian, anyone?), and some pretty sexy prose. Speaking of, experts say that reading romance books could even improve your sex life.

Shavon Gaddy-Dalrymple, a licensed psychotherapist who works with couples and polycules to improve sexual and sensual intimacy, believes romance novels “are great at tantalizing the reader with long, drawn out scenes of foreplay which can improve one’s ideas around sensuality, temptation, and longing.” She also adds, “In most cases, romance novels are basically better-written porn scenes complete with a fully-formed plot and well-developed characters.” Romance authors agree. “Everyone loves love. That’s what we all want in this mixed up world.”

Some guys are already catching on to the joys of the romance genre. A 2017 study from the Romance Writers of America found that 18 percent of romance readers are men, and one-third of erotic audiobook downloaders are also guys. The majority of romance readers are still women, yes, but with more publishers focusing on LGBTQ+ experiences and expanding past the traditional heteronormative girl-meets-guy plot, publishers predict male readership will only increase.

Todd Stocke, senior vice president and editorial director of Sourcebooks (whose authors include bestsellers E L James, Ana Huang, and more), weighed in on the growing market. “There is zero question that the romance industry is growing in inclusivity,” he says. “The LGBTQIA+ romance space has changed completely, with a dramatically-expanded author base, much broader and more exciting storytelling, authors of all stripes writing with authentic representation, and a progressive readership that, yes, includes more men.”

'Neon Gods' by Katee Robert

There are many worlds to fall into with author Katee Robert. From modernized Greek myths to monster romances, she’s basically the god of sexual and delightful tales rolled into one. Her explosive bestseller, Neon Gods, is a Hades and Persephone retelling that’s heavy on the raunch, and is the first book of her Dark Olympus series.

In the fast-paced and modern city of Olympus, the society’s darling Persephone isn’t glamoured by the politics and deceit of the elite. She’s forced into an engagement to none other than Zeus—the top god of the city’s reigning Thirteen Houses. Her only way to escape is to make a deal with the black sheep and dark underlord of the Thirteen, Hades himself. He’s all too happy to help Persephone, especially if it means a bit of revenge on the golden ones. Except after spending one night in bed with Persephone, his revenge becomes muddled into a much deeper desire.

'Red, White & Royal Blue' by Casey McQuiston

New York Times bestselling author Casey McQuiston’s romantic comedy, Red, White and Royal Blue is a celebratory story of love between the First Son of the United States and the Prince of England. There’s plenty of wit and steamy scenes in this debut from McQuiston, which is already receiving the royal treatment from Hollywood—a movie based on the book is expected to premiere August 11 on Amazon Prime.

When the son of the U.S. President Alexander Claremont-Diaz and his sister June attend a royal wedding in the United Kingdom, Alex clumsily pushes Prince Henry into a wedding cake. The debacle kicks off a media uproar forcing the prince and first son into a series of damage control events. They develop an unlikely friendship, which then turns more romantic, causing them both to question their duties, true selves, and family.

'Before I Let Go' by Kennedy Ryan

Kennedy Ryan is frequently compared to bestselling author Colleen Hoover, and for good reason. She writes heart-wrenching romances that are sexy and modern. Before I Let Go is a second-chance romance that shines a light on the benefits of therapy for men as the love interest, Josiah, struggles with his mental health. He’s resistant to therapy in the beginning, but he gives it a chance for his young son and is shocked when he starts to get something out of it. The therapy brings Josiah into a place where he's ready to seek out his ex-wife, and hopefully begin anew.

'Seven Days in June' by Tia Williams

Tia William’s Seven Days in June, a New York Times bestseller and Reese Witherspoon book club pick, is perfect for anyone who believes in second chances. The book centers on Eva Mercy, a bestselling erotica writer, and Shane Hall, a reclusive award-winning novelist, who meet unexpectedly at a literary event in New York. They’re clearly attracted to one another, but what no one knows is that Shane and Eva have a romantic past and both have been secretly writing to each other through their books all of these years. Eva’s afraid of the all-encompassing attraction she feels for Shane, but for the next seven days during a sweltering Brooklyn summer, the writers are given a second shot at love.

'The Deal' by Elle Kennedy

With fans like the USC Hockey Team and the Philadelphia Phillies' Bryce Harper, you’ll be in solid book club company while reading bestselling author Elle Kennedy. The first book in her sexy Off-Campus series is about two Briar University students Garrett Graham, a cocky hockey player, and Type-A academic Hannah Wells, steaming up the ice. Hannah has most of her life figured out, except she’s insecure when it comes to men. Wanting to get the attention of her crush, she’s willing to do just about anything, including appearing more desirable by faux-dating the annoying captain of the hockey team, Garrett, who she’s tutoring. Garrett’s GPA is crashing, putting his spot on the team in jeopardy, and he makes a deal with the pretty and not-failing-the-ethics-course brunette, Hannah, if it means free tutoring. An unexpected kiss between the two leads to a night of sex worthy of the Stanley Cup, and it gets pretty wild from there.

If you somehow escaped watching the first two seasons of the popular Bridgerton series on Netflix, then all you’ve truly accomplished is missing out on some fabulous sex scenes. But before Regé-Jean Page and Phoebe Dynevor titillated Netflix viewers, Julia Quinn, author of the Bridgerton series, has been writing smart and sexy romance reads since the ‘90s.

The Bridgerton series are eight standalone Regency romance novels that follow the wealthy Bridgerton family in London’s high society. The Duke and I, the first in the series, follows the eldest daughter of the Bridgerton family, Daphne, as she navigates the politics of the marriage scene, recruiting the curtly-spoken and seemingly unattainable Duke of Hastings to be her faux love interest in a mutually beneficial arrangement. Despite the lack of communication and hidden secrets, Daphne and Simon fall for one another while London society is buzzing about a secret writer dishing out gossip on everyone in town.

Notting Hill meets Almost Famous in this sweet rom-com from Matthew Norman. 'Kingdom' by W.S. Greer

W.S. Greer is the author of bestselling novels Claiming Carter, Kingpin (an Italian mafia romance), and The Therapist Series. A Renaissance man who writes spicy romance, and is also a USAF veteran who's still serving today. Greer is a prolific writer, with Kingdom being his 17th published book—and it’s an intense one.

Heavy on the BDSM, Kingdom is about self-discovery and a sexual awakening of Greer’s protagonist, Alina. After a terrible breakup, Alina’s friends surprise her with a meet-and-greet with her favorite author, Nathan Booker. She’s desperate to meet him because Booker doesn’t just write your average romance books...his books heavily feature a lifestyle she’s fascinated by, but has never indulged in. When the author and Alina meet, they begin an intense, deliciously-twisted romance. It’s one of the hotter books on this list.

'Verity' by Colleen Hoover

Verity comes recommended by Lad Sam, one of The Literary Lads (a book club comprised of men that read popular romance) of Instagram fame. 'You Made a Fool of Death with Your Beauty' by Akwaeke Emezi

One of last year's most anticipated books, You Made a Fool of Death with Your Beauty is from the New York Times bestselling author Akwaeke Emezi. Five years have passed since Feyi Adekola’s heart was shattered when an accident killed the love of her life. She’s almost a completely different person after the event, living as an artist in a brownstone with her best friend, Joy, who encourages Feyi to start dating again. But Feyi isn’t looking for another all-encompassing love...maybe a fling? A sexy rooftop party one night leads to a whirlwind experience, jetting off to a luxurious tropical island in the fabulous home of a queer celebrity chef, Alim. Except Feyi finds herself attached to an older man she shouldn’t be, and is also inexplicably and widely attracted to Alim, who also understands the complex feelings of her grief.

'Melody of Midnight' by Angela J. Ford

Persephone meets Snow White in this gothic-inspired dark fantasy romance from indie author Angela J. Ford, which has plenty of spice and action. When the conniving queen kills Princess Celeste’s father, she’s convinced she’ll be the next one targeted. Fleeing for her life into the forbidden forest, Celeste becomes hopelessly lost. She meets a half-man, half-monster who offers her shelter, but at a price—a vow to help him in his own quest, and in return he promises to protect her from the queen. As Celeste fulfills her vow, she’s thrown into a treacherous world of magic, secrets, and music, including a castle full of gnomes, an underground mine, and portals guarded by menacing creatures. She also finds herself falling deeply for the King of Hearts.

'Honey & Spice' by Bolu Babalola

Bolu Babalola’s debut romance, Honey and Spice, is “sexy, messy and wry,” as described by The New York Times Book Review, with plenty of swoon-worthy moments. Babalola's exuberant contemporary romance follows the sharp-tongued (but secretly tenderhearted) Kiki Banjo, host of a popular student radio show at Whitewell University called "Brown Sugar." Kiki has been focused on convincing her listeners to not fall for manipulative men, but when she kisses Malakai Korede, someone she publicly denounced as “The Wastemen of Whitewell,” in front of every Blackwellian on campus, her reputation and show might crumble. Malakai is exactly the sort of charming guy Kiki has warned her listeners about—he’s funny, cocky, but also endearing in a silly way—and she just can’t resist him.

'The Shape of Us' by Drew Davies

The Shape of Us is a romantic comedy set in modern day London with several intertwining and unique love stories. Daisy is rushing to work when a handsome stranger on a bike almost knocks her over, and when he doesn't, he asks for her number instead. On the same day, JoJo, a wife in her 60s, is trying to win her philandering husband back from his mistress. Then there’s Adam, who has recently lost his job and lies to his housemate about where he goes every day, fearing he’ll be evicted if the truth comes out. And finally, there’s Dylan, a teenage boy who lives with an illness that keeps him in his bedroom—but hasn’t stopped him falling hopelessly in love. These four strangers eventually cross paths and find they all have one thing in common.

'Jasmine and Jake Rock the Boat' by Sonya Lalli

Sonya Lalli, who wrote the delightful A Holly Jolly Diwali, has yet again created a vivid cast of characters that you’ll be thinking about for a while. Her latest is an enemies-to-lovers romance titled Jasmine and Jake Rock the Boat—an utterly charming romp filled with lively quips and plenty of humor.

Jasmine Randhawa had the perfect life in Seattle, a good-looking boyfriend, and a great job, but when her relationship abruptly ends she makes an impulsive decision to tag along on her parent’s Alaskan cruise vacation. What’s worse is the cruise isn’t really for everyone; it’s a senior’s cruise and the only other person under 50 is the cocky Jake Dhillon. Jasmine and Jake are quick to clash, but over the 10 days at sea, the tide changes and the pair have noticed their usual anxiety and cynicism about love disappears whenever they’re together.

'From Blood and Ash' by Jennifer L. Armentrout

Jennifer Armentrout is a bestselling author who writes contemporary romance, new adult, and fantasy—and perhaps her most well-known series to date is the dark fantasy romance, From Blood and Ash. The series features gods, primals, drakons (yes, drakons), and shapeshifting zombie-like beings with snake-infested bodies. And a ton of spice. There are several love interests and a hefty dollop of sex scenes. Her prequel series, A Shadow in the Ember, is incredible—especially if you’re tired of the seduced-maiden plot and would rather it be the other way around. It’s also just as bloody and action packed as From Blood and Ash. It was recently announced that Amazon will be turning Armentrout’s series into a show, tapping Anne Cofell Saunders (The Boys, Battlestar Galactica, and more) as the showrunner.

'Romantic Comedy' by Curtis Sittenfeld

Freshly published this April, Curtis Sittenfeld’s latest, Romantic Comedy, is receiving high praise from fans. The New York Times bestseller is about a comedy writer who has sworn off love until she happens to meet a handsome pop star on a comedy show. Sally Milz, a writer for The Night Owls, a late-night live comedy show similar to SNL, is over love. She creates a skit mocking the recent relationship between one of her co-writers dubbed the “Danny Horst Rule,” after discovering the moderately attractive Danny is dating a beautiful celebrity—a phenomenon she thinks is unlikely for women, but happens often for men.

Enter Noah Brewster, a handsome pop star with a reputation for dating models, set to host this week’s show, which includes Sally’s skit. Surprisingly, the usually undazzled Sally finds herself easily collaborating with Noah on the sketch prior to the show. She begins to wonder, is it the skit, or is it her that’s tied up in a Danny Horst moment? And does Noah feel the same? Sittenfeld's book is a hilarious and sophisticated take on love and relationships. It's packed with sharp observations that delve into the often overwhelming and anxiety-inducing feelings that come with falling in love, all while dissecting the social rituals and gender relations of modern romance. If you quote Tina Fey’s 30 Rock at parties (guilty) this is a must-read.
