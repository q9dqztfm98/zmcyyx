# The Nike Epic React Flyknit Sneakers Will Make You Feel Fast as Hell

[Will Make You Feel](https://tiancansz.com/e9xeyzny/)

It’s been awhile since I’ve felt this . . . fast.

This was the first thought that entered my mind when I began running in the Nike Epic React Flyknit. It was this past Saturday, and I was on a Trueform nonmotorized treadmill, working through some sprint work, just experimenting with Nike’s newest running shoe.

Nike’s last running shoe, 2017’s Zoom Fly, was a solid entry, but in situations like this one, it had failed. The Zoom Fly had been a limited shoe, impressive at long distances, but far from potent at helping your finishing kick or letting you utilize an explosive stride.

There are no such problems with the Epic React Flyknit. Nike’s newest runner, which hits stores in late February, is its most versatile and responsive running shoe to date, the kind of shoe that lets you run without thinking. Every step you take in the Flyknits is a steady, sure, confident one, thanks to smart design, and the strong React sole.

When we last saw the React soles last year, Nike was introducing them in its latest Hyperdunk basketball shoes. In those sneakers, the goal of the React soles was to “react” to your foot as you ran and jumped and cut around the basketball court, lending stability and the slightest bit of lift when you elevated.

In the Epic React Flyknits, Nike merges that stability into a sneaker that you barely notice even exists. The Flyknits are incredibly light and terrifically breathable, so you don them and hop on a treadmill or hit the pavement and you feel liberated, as if wearing nothing. Despite that, unlike most minimalist running shoes, the Flyknits offer tremendous stability and security; your foot feels balanced and safe in them, allowing you to attack curves and alter your stride with ease.

The Flyknit upper ensures that your foot stays cool during all that running, too. This is a terrifically breathable pair of running shoes, although the upper is almost too thin at times; wear the wrong color socks, and you just may see them right through the sneakers. That was all kinds of underrated cool when it came to the Hyperdunks, but on the Epic React Flyknits, it doesn’t seem as stylish.

[Will Make You Feel](https://leanorbit.dns0663.com/blog/4858983707.html)

Still, that’s a minor quibble because the rest of the sneaker is so strong and well-constructed. Nike doesn’t just graft the React soles onto these things, either; it delivers smart touches to make your running ride feel supreme. The overall look is sleep and eye-catching, driven by bright colors along the back side of the shoe. A traction pattern on the bottom helps you grip the running surface, particularly near your toes and on the back heel.

It’s a smooth riding experience overall, and it’s one that Nike promises will last you. The hallmark of the React soles is supposed to be their durability: Most sneaker soles wear out after a while, and you can feel it in a few months. In theory, the React soles are supposed to last longer, standing the test of miles upon miles. Your mileage is going to vary greatly here, though, depending on the style of your stride. My React Flyknits have instantly jumped to the top of my list of favorite running shoes, in large part because they feel so electrically fast and secure for sprint work. But the bottoms of the soles are already showing some signs of wear on their outsides. That’s typical of most running shoes, for me, though, so you can’t hold that against the Epic React Flyknits too much.

Especially not when the rest of the package is so good that they’ll leave you feeling this fast.
