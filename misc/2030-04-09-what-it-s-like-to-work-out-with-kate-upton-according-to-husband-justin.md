Justin Verlander's Workout

When Verlander heads to the gym, he spends a lot of time on mobility, getting ready for his lifts. "I love mobility. It’s actually one of my favorite parts of working out, just because it’s so important," he said. (Try out this mobility exercise to improve your squat.)

His job as an MLB pitcher also requires a lot of core strength and overall power. For that, he turns to medicine ball slams. "Ball slams kind of simulate what I do, so I do them quite a bit… 7 to 10 reps at a time, just as hard as I can," he said.

While the 35-year-old said he doesn’t do quite as much strength training as he used to when he was younger, it’s still one of his favorite aspects of working out. His go-to lift? Hex bar deadlifts. They may not share their workouts, but he and his wife seem to agree hex bar deadlifts are a great lift, considering she regularly crushes them with her trainer, Ben Bruno.

Preparing for the Upcoming Season

Now that spring is in the air, Verlander is starting to tone down on the strength training and focus more on maintenance for the upcoming season. But with that comes another challenge no amount of training (or yelling from Upton) can solve: seasonal allergies.

"I have suffered from allergies a long time," Verlander said. "It’s pretty easy to understand how allergies could impact someone. If I have itchy eyes or can’t breathe when I’m on the mound, I’m definitely not going to be performing at my best."

Which is why Verlander partnered with Flonase, which has ensured brutal allergy symptoms are the last thing on his mind while he’s playing. “I use it almost everyday, and have no problems,” he said.

As for Verlander and Upton, the couple is an excellent example of how two people can be #swolemates without sharing a workout.

"We complement each other pretty well," Verlander said. "We do very different workouts, but it’s fun. I like being there to support her."

Especially if that means yelling at her during sled pushes.
