# The Back-Saving Workout

Exercise can be an antidote to a variety of ills, but you have to know how to invest your sweat. For example, according to a new University of Alberta study, strength-training is better than cardio at relieving lower back pain. Researchers found that people who hit the weights for 4 months experienced a 63 percent reduction in pain, while those who did only aerobic exercise felt no improvement. The difference is likely due to the total-body benefits of lifting. “By adding muscular strength to your whole body, you reduce the fatigue that builds up throughout the day,” explains study author Rob Kell, Ph.D., “and you’re better able to maintain good posture, which minimizes stress on your lower back.”

Thanks to Dr. Kell and his colleagues, we’ve reproduced the exact workout followed by people in the study. Check with your doctor before starting the exercise program, then read on.

The exercises: Leg press 1. Sit in the leg press machine with your bottom and back straight against the seat of the machine. Line your feet up approximately shoulder width apart on the foot platform, and grasp the handles. 2. Release the foot platform and gradually lower the weights until your knees bend at a 90-degree angle. 3. Pause, and then push the platform back to the starting position. Repeat.

Leg extension 1. Situate your body in the leg-extension machine so your back and bottom are flush against the perpendicular pad and your shins press gently against the leg pads. Your hips and knees should bend 90 degrees and your ankles should rest under the footpads, also bent at 90 degrees. 2. Extend both legs slowly until they are almost horizontal to the ground. 3. Pause, and then return the weights to the starting position. Repeat.

Leg curl 1. Arrange yourself facedown on the leg-curl machine so the back of your lower legs are against the cushion, above your heels and below your calves. 2. Stay flat on the machine, then bend your legs at the knees and draw your heels to you as much as you can, lifting the weight. 3. Pause, and then lower the weight back to the starting position. Repeat.

Bench press 1. Lie on your back on a level bench and clutch the bar with an overhand grasp just past shoulder-width. Raise the bar so it’s above your chin. 2. Lower it to your chest, pause briefly, then push the weight back to the starting position. Repeat.

Barbell incline bench press 1. Lie on your back on an incline bench and place your feet flush on the floor. Grasp the bar with your hands out slightly past shoulder-width, and extend it up over your chest. 2. Gradually lower the bar until it meets your chest just above your nipples, pause, and then extend the bar back up to the start position. Repeat.

Lat pulldown 1. Clutch the bar with an overhand grip, just past your shoulders. 2. Pull the bar down to the top of your chest (do not lean back excessively). 3. Pause, and then return to the starting position. Repeat.

Dumbbell shoulder press 1. Sit on a bench with two dumbbells. Hold them at the sides of your shoulders so that your arms bend and your palms face forward. 2. Push the weights directly above your head, pause, and then slowly lower them back to the start position. Repeat.

Straight bar arm curl 1. Grip the bar at arm's length in front of your thighs with an underhand hold about shoulder-width apart. 2. Steady your upper arms and keep them still as you gradually curl the bar to about shoulder height. 3. Slowly lower the barbell back to the starting position. Repeat.

Triceps pushdown 1. Face the triceps pushdown machine and place your hands on the bar with an overhand grip. The bar should rest at about chest height. 2. Hold your elbows close to your sides. Your feet should be shoulder-width apart and slightly bent. 3. Push the bar down until your arms are completely extended and your elbows are almost (but not quite) straight. Make sure that you keep your back straight. 4. Slowly release the pressure on the wire and let the weights return to the starting position. Repeat.

Abdominal crunch 1. Lie on your back, with your feet flat on the floor and knees bent. Place your hands on the side of your head (ear level). 2. Lift your head and shoulders and crunch your rib cage toward your pelvis. 3. Pause, and slowly return to the starting position. Repeat.

Swiss ball crunch 1. Carefully sit on the Swiss ball (like a chair without a back), then slowly walk your feet forward so that you are laying on your back over top of the Swiss ball. Place your hands on the side of your head (ear level). 2. Lift your head and shoulders and crunch your rib cage toward your pelvis. 3. Pause, and slowly return to the starting position. Repeat.

Prone superman 1. Lie on your stomach with your legs straight out behind you and your arms extended straight ahead of you, with your hands palm down on the floor. 2. Lift your arms, head, chest, and calves off the floor at the same time. 3. Maintain this position for 5 to 30 seconds, while keeping your head and neck at the same elevation as your shoulders. 4. Return to the starting position with your arms, head, chest, and calves resting back on the floor. Repeat.
