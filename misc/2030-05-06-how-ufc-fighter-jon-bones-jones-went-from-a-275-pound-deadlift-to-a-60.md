# How UFC Fighter Jon “Bones” Jones Went From a 275-Pound Deadlift to a 600-Pound Deadlift in 15 Months

Jon “Bones” Jones lost it all.

The top-ranked pound-for-pound UFC fighter and light heavyweight champion destroyed his rivals for six years with flying knee kicks and spinning elbow strikes.

Then, following a conviction for felony hit-and-run in 2015, UFC stripped Jones of his title. That same year, he also tested positive for cocaine, and allegedly competed in a drag race.

Jones, 28, spent 15 months doing community service, getting sober, and finding himself—in part by committing to more strength training. Now he can squat 500 pounds and deadlift 600—both up from 275 pounds—and do a 55-inch box jump.

This Saturday, when the 6’4”, 220-pound Jones steps into the Octagon to fight Ovince Saint Preux at UFC 197, he’ll be stronger than ever before.

In this candid interview with Men’s Health contributor and CNN New Day anchor Chris Cuomo, Jones dishes on his many mistakes, new outlook, and journey to strength.

Men’s Health: What do you learn when you lose so much?

Jon Jones: When I look back on 2015, the things that I lost—my belt, endorsements, money, respect—don’t come close to getting my sobriety back and gaining the opportunity to re-evaluate who I am as a person and where my life is going.

I got my freaking mind back. The things I lost can be re-earned, but getting yourself back? There’s no price. There’s no belt that compares. I’m so grateful.

You had to do a lot of community service with kids. What did you tell them?

I told them when you mess up, it’s important to not give up on yourself. If you do something bad, it doesn’t mean you’re a bad person. It means you had bad judgment.

We’re all going to make mistakes; they’re inevitable. It’s what you do after these mistakes that matters.

How hard was it for you to change your friend circle?

It was really tough. When I got sober, I realized I really didn’t have as many friends as I thought. A lot of the people who I hung out with in my personal life were guys who like to drink and smoke pot.

So when I decided to go full steam ahead with sobriety, I knew in order to do it successfully I would have to change my circle. For a month or two I went through this awkward stage of boredom—not really knowing what to do, or who to call. I was lonely.

One day when I was bored, I just went down to a powerlifting gym, Via Strength Systems in Albuquerque. I knew I needed to expend my energy somehow.

I started working out with them four days a week. I became obsessed with lifting and being fit. It’s paid off. Right now I’m physically stronger than I’ve ever been.

You won all your championships without being into fitness?

Yeah, absolutely. I took things for granted. I abused my gifts. Now I can more clearly see what an opportunity it is to be the best in the world at something.

Some of the best athletes say the scariest competitor is your own expectation.

It’s intimidating if you truly give your all. I’ve always felt like if I was to somehow lose, I would know that I hadn’t given it 100 percent.

There was always some mental leeway that I could give more. It was a safety net to party, drink, and smoke. I’d know that I wasn’t at my best.

Now that I’m sober and working my butt off in the offseason, I feel more vulnerable. What I’m facing now is the fear of giving my best.

What happened when you walked into Via Strength Systems?

When I arrived, I was one of the weakest guys. I was surrounded by guys who weighed 300 pounds and lifted just short of 1000 pounds. On every team I’ve ever been on, I’ve been one of the better guys.

But it was different being around powerlifters. I’m a tall, skinny fighter! I was dedicated and focused. I started getting better and better.

When I first started deadlifting, I could lift 275. Seven months later I’m up to 600 pounds. I first started squatting around 275, but now I’m at 500.

Holy cow. How much weight did you gain?

I started around 230 pounds, but I was pretty out of shape. I got up to 238 pounds—but I had visible abs. I transformed a lot of fat into muscle.

Fitness really changed my life. Weightlifting is my new high.

How has strength training made you a better fighter?

I’m not smoking anymore, so my cardio is great! I jump higher and my knees fly through the air faster with less effort.

When I shoot in and finish a wrestling takedown, [my opponent] goes over so much easier. I used to be really skinny, so I had to work extra hard to get a takedown. I had to really focus on my timing to catch a guy off guard. Now when I’m out of position, I can just use pure strength to make certain things happen.

Even my balance feels better. I punch faster. I punch harder. I’ve never gotten a knockout in the UFC, but if I knock out my next opponent I won’t really be surprised.

I’m stronger than some of the heavyweights on our team, so my confidence is really through the roof.

The guys at the gym call me Meat.
