# 10 Walton Goggins Shows and Movies to Watch After 'The White Lotus'

WHILE MUCH HAS been said and argued about season 3 of The White Lotus, one thing is up for little debate: The show's ensemble cast was fantastic across the board. And one member of the show's cast who particularly stuck out was Mr. Walton Goggins, the 53-year-old, Alabama-born, Georgia-raised actor who played the doomed, vengeful vacationer Rick Hatchett. Viewers were captivated by Goggins's performance all season, whether it be in his doomed romance with Chelsea (Aimee Lou Wood), his bizarre and debauchery-filled friendship with Frank (Sam Rockwell), or his much-anticipated confrontation with Jim Hollinger (Scott Glenn). The White Lotus creator Mike White asked a lot of Goggins, and he delivered a performance that dominated the screen and made a major, major impact.

The role as Rick puts an exclamation point on what Goggins has been doing in his career, across television and film, for more than 30 years. While his profile has increased significantly in recent years thanks to a handful of recent projects, Goggins has always been someone with a wide range of ability on screen; He can make you laugh, he can make you cry, and he can make you scared. He's the kind of guy who an play a terrifying villain, and turn him around and make him into an anti-hero you're rooting for much in the same way. There aren't many actors out there who have the energy and charisma that Walton Goggins does, and he makes great use of it in any project that affords him the chance.

Another thing that makes Goggins such a unique presence in the film and television landscape is that he kind of shows up in... well, everything. Early in his career, he popped up in small roles in movies like The Next Karate Kid, Major League: Back to tehe Minors, and Shanghai Noon. Later, he would appear in genre staples like Rob Zombie's House of 1000 Corpses or in small roles for significant filmmakers like Spike Lee's Miracle at St. Anna, or Steven Spielberg's Lincoln; He even had a knack for appearing in some key roles in blockbusters like Cowboys and Aliens, Ant-Man and the Wasp, and Tomb Raider(2018). Which is all to say... before you knew Walton Goggins, there's a pretty decent chance you still knew him without even realizing it. Hell, he even almost appeared in a recent L.A. Confidential reboot series (alongside fellow guy you definitely know Shea Whigham) before the pilot wasn't picked up for series (Oh, what could've been!).

But we know what you want from him. Below, we've pulled together a list of of 10 movies and shows that capture Mr. Goggins in his most Goggins-ish form. We're talking intense some times, scary other times, and hilarious in between—and charismatic throughout it all. If you're still reeling from the end of The White Lotus, don't worry, because there's way more Walton Goggins where that came from.

You always kind of get to do more when you're the villain. And in Justified, Goggins plays the foil—a lowlife criminal named Boyd Crowder—to Timothy Olyphant's lead U.S. Marhsal Raylan Givens. Goggins was originally only supposed to appear in the show's very first episode, but he's so good—and has such fantastic chemistry with Olyphant, that he became a fixture in the show for the duration of its six-season run. The Righteous Gemstones (2019-2025)

It would be safe to say that the two most important actor relationships in Goggins's career have been with Timothy Olyphant (who we already discussed above with regard to Justified) and with Danny McBride. While their first collaboration, Vice Principals, is also a brilliant piece of work (and we'll discuss it further below, we promise), it's The Righteous Gemstones that allows Goggins to play what is by far the funniest, most outlandish, and perhaps brilliant character of his career in "Baby" Billy Freeman, a totally absurd, greedy, narcissistic old man televangelist who is in a constant struggle with his brother-in-law (John Goodman) and his kids (McBride, Adam Devine, Edi Patterson) after the death of his sister (Jennifer Nettles). Think Succession through Danny McBride's one-of-a-kind comedy lens—and give Goggins several Emmys that he for some reason hasn't even been nominated for.

It goes without question that the chance to work with a legendary filmmaker and a legendary cast (which also includes Jamie Foxx, Samuel L. Vice Principals (2016 - 2017)

And we arrive back at Vice Principals. In Danny McBride and Walton Goggins's first series together, they play a pair of high school teachers (the titular Vice Principals) who are competing for the school's top job... and team up when an outsider (Kimberly Hébert Gregory) gets the job. It was clear just from watching McBride's Neil Gamby (a stupid man) and Goggins's Lee Russell (an absolute sociopath) that these two had comedic chemistry that you don't come across often. Vice Principals is only two perfect seasons long (by design), and you will watch and eat this up very quickly.

The Shield (2002 - 2008)

You've heard of police dramas on TV, but The Shield is unlike just about any of them. Part of the initial wave of prestige TV (a contemporary of shows like The Sopranos, The Wire, and Deadwood), The Shield follows Detective Vic Mackey (Michael Chikilis) who leads the corrupt and criminal strike team to carry out their police work; Goggins plays one of the team's detectives, Shane Vendrell, who is also Vic's best friend. The show is a brutal exploration of unethical police behavior and is based in part on the real-life Rampart police scandal. This is probably the darkest of Goggins's many extended TV roles, but with 7 seasons and 88 episodes, is one that you can really sink your teeth into as well.

Fallout (2024 - )

If Goggins had a true breakout role prior to The White Lotus, it may have been when he played The Ghoul/Cooper Howard on the video game adaptation Fallout, which debuted in 2024. This post-apocalyptic thriller was one of the year's best shows, a watchable mystery with great world-building and even better characters, but Goggins's Cooper was without question one of the highlights. A gunslinging, amoral monster with a heartbreaking backstory, he's a classic kind of character who viewers always want to root for. We're eagerly awaiting season 2.

I'm A Virgo (2023)

Perhaps the most underseen project on this list is I'm A Virgo, which was writer/director Boots Riley's follow-up to his excellent 2018 debut film Sorry To Bother You. Much like that film, I'm A Virgo is a heavily satirical but super-engaging story that takes place in a heightened, magical realism world; Jharrel Jeromeplays a giant man named Cootie in our normal world, while Goggins, in a parallel storyline, plays a military-state superhero named Jay Whittle, or, The Hero, who is kind of like a combination of RoboCop, Tony Stark, and Guy Pearce's Iron Man 3 villain—and also kind of sick in the head. Cootie is also a fan of The Hero and the comics and stories told about him, which makes the collision course that these two are on throughout the entire show particularly troubling. Riley is one of our best thinkers when it comes to social themes and ideas, and isn't afraid to get abstract with it either. I'm A Virgo is one of the most unique and strange shows you'll watch, and Goggins is great as usual in his limited role.

Predators (2010)

Predators was released in 2010, 20 years after the release of what was the most recent entry in the franchise at the time, Predator 2. And while this movie doesn't have the strongest reputation in 2025, it's actually a quite underrated and fun entry in the famed sci-fi action franchise. The premise of this one is pretty simple: A bunch of killers are dropped on a planet to be hunted by a bunch of Predators, and Goggins plays one of those killers seeking to survive. It's kind of like The Running Man but with Predators (the title, according to producer Robert Rodriguez, is a nod to James Cameron's 1986 staple Aliens). Does any part of that sound not awesome? Goggins is part of a cast that also includes Adrien Brody, Laurence Fishburne, and Topher Grace, among others.

Invincible (2021 - )

Part of what makes Invincible such a fun and unique take on the superhero genre is that it doesn't go full satire in its depiction of heroes and villains, but rather features an untethered world where there is no limit on violence or any kindof behavior. What happens in Invincible isn't parody, but rather a subversion of the way you may expect these stories to play out. Goggins plays one of his quietest characters—Cecil Stedman, who is essentially Invincible's version of Nick Fury. In turn, he's also one of the most important parts of the show—and someone who's behavior you're constantly thinking about and analyzing how and why they do what they do.
