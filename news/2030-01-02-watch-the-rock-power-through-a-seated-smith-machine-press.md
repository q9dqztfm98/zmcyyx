# Watch The Rock Power Through a Seated Smith Machine Press

Dwayne "The Rock" Johnson has been sharing insights into his recent superhero body transformation throughout production of DC's Black Adam. Don't expect him to stop, just because the movie has now wrapped.

In recent Instagram video, Johnson offered a look off his seated shoulder press, churning out the reps on a Smith machine to 'Enter Sandman' by Metallica (heavy metal is a prevalent genre on the actor's "Iron Paradise" playlist). But while the Smith machine has a reputation as a cop-out among some corners of the fitness community, as it keeps the movement on a fixed path, nobody can accuse The Rock of not putting in the work.

For one, it's worth noting that as an action star, Johnson is training towards a specific aesthetic goal, rather than for functional fitness. As such, his objective is not necessarily to take the overhead press through the full range of motion, but rather to keep tension on his deltoids.

"Don't think of it as an overhead press; think of it as a 'machine shoulder press,' and then it makes sense," says Men's Health fitness director Ebenezer Samuel C.S.C.S., who also points out that Johnson is using a lighter weight than we might expect, around 90 to 100 pounds, and is clearly going for volume of repetitions in the video.

"You don't want to overload your overhead presses in a Smith machine, partly because your shoulders don't have much freedom to move in their natural arc," he explains. "He's not overloading it, compared to what we've seen him do on bench presses, other exercises. He's using a light weight and that's also key when doing any restrictive machine press: Chase the pump on these machines, not PRs. Take a lesson from one of the biggest, baddest men to ever do it: A machine isn't the time to show off how much weight you can do."
