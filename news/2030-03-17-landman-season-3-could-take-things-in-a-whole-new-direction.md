WE'VE MADE IT through two seasons of Landman, and, honestly, we're starting to feel a bit of whiplash (and, in this case, that's quite complimentary). Taylor Sheridan's one-of-a-kind oil man drama has burned through all kinds of drama in its two go-arounds so far, ranging from the oil drilling storylines that we expected to the casino, pool therapy, and college roommate situations that we couldn't possibly have seen coming. And somehow, in the grander context of Landman, it all kind of just fits together in this world. It's hard to really explain, but Sheridan's shows sometimes have a knack for making the oddest choices and turns of events feel like a bit of a masterstroke.

It helps that he consistently pulls in some of the most talented casts on all of television. Seriously—Landman season 2 lost one of the greats of the medium in Jon Hamm (he of Mad Men fame), but casually replaced him with a trio of Oscar-nominated legends (in Sam Elliott's new character and increased roles for Demi Moore and Andy Garcia). And that's not even counting our cool-as-a-cucumber leading man, Billy Bob Thornton (himself a legend of both the big and small screen). It's fun to watch these people on our TVs, no matter what it is they're doing.

As Landman moves toward its third season, there seem to be some major changes, particularly in the workplace for Tommy (Thornton), after being fired by Cami (Moore) in the back end of Landman season 2. But as we saw in the season 2 finale, Tommy isn't on board with smoothly settling into a comfy job with Chevron. He wants to hit one last big home run, and so he goes into business with all of his friends and family (more or less) to create their own oil company: CTT Oil Exploration and Cattle.

No, no cattle will be involved. No, it makes sense to absolutely no one. Just roll with it! We're just going to roll with it too, because it presents a whole new idea and vibe for Landman as it moves toward season 3.

Will there be a Landman season 3?

There will indeed be a Landman season 3, as the show was renewed for a third season back at the start of December 2025.

This comes as no surprise, as the show has been a huge, buzzy hit for Paramount+ ever since its season 1 debut a year ago. And with Sheridan departing Paramount for his new $1 Billion deal with NBC/Universal, it's likely that Paramount+ will want to hold onto his existing hits for the time being, as long as they keep on drawing an audience and being at least moderately successful.
