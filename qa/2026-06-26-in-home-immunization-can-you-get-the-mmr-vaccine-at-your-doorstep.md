The MMR vaccine helps prevent three dangerous sicknesses: measles, mumps, and rubella. This vaccine is an important one that should not be overlooked. Most people visit their doctor’s office to get vaccines, but with the rise in in-home healthcare, you may be able to get an MMR vaccine in the comfort of your own home.

This blog post explores the possibilities of getting the MMR vaccine at home and any potential risks and benefits.

Understanding the MMR Vaccine

MMR stands for measles, mumps, and rubella. The MMR vaccine prevents the infection and spread of these three conditions. According to The National Institute of Health, the MMR vaccine is highly effective in preventing these illnesses, showing 99% in preventing measles after a second vaccination and 95% in preventing mumps.

The MMR vaccine is typically given as two different shots. The first shot is around 12 to 15 months, and the second is given between the ages of 4 and 6.
