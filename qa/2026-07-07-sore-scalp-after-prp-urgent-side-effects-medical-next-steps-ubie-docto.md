Why Is My Scalp Sore?

A sore scalp can be more than just an annoyance—it may signal an underlying issue that needs attention. Whether you're experiencing itching, burning, tenderness, or all of the above, understanding the possible causes can help you take the right next steps.

Common Causes of Scalp Soreness

Mechanical irritation• Tight hairstyles (braids, ponytails)• Rough brushing or scratching• Frequent use of heat tools

Dermatologic conditions• Seborrheic dermatitis (dandruff)• Psoriasis• Eczema

Infections• Folliculitis (inflamed hair follicles)• Ringworm (scalp tinea)

Allergic reactions• Shampoos, conditioners, hair dyes• Hair styling products

Underlying medical issues• Nutritional deficiencies (iron, vitamin D)• Hormonal changes• Stress or scalp tension

If your discomfort is mild and short-lived, changes to your hair care routine—gentler products, looser styles, less heat—may be all you need. But if soreness persists, worsens, or you develop additional symptoms (fever, swelling, pus), it's time to seek medical advice.

PRP for Hair Loss: What You Need to Know

[Medical Next](https://copperglow.dns0663.com/)

Platelet-Rich Plasma (PRP) therapy has gained popularity as a treatment for hair thinning and early-stage hair loss. By harnessing your own blood's growth factors, PRP aims to revitalize dormant hair follicles and promote thicker hair growth.

How PRP Treatment Works

Blood DrawA small sample of your blood is taken, similar to a routine lab test.

CentrifugationThe sample is spun in a centrifuge to separate platelets and plasma from red blood cells.

InjectionThe concentrated platelets are injected into the scalp in areas of thinning or hair loss.

Follow-UpYou typically need several sessions (often 3–4, spaced 4–6 weeks apart) and occasional maintenance treatments.

Benefits of PRP for Hair Loss

Uses your own blood—no foreign substances

Minimally invasive, outpatient procedure

Minimal downtime—most people return to normal activities immediately

PRP for Hair Loss Side Effects

While PRP is generally well tolerated, it's important to know about potential side effects and be prepared.

Temporary Scalp Soreness• Mild to moderate discomfort at injection sites• Typically peaks 24–48 hours after treatment

Bruising or Swelling• Minor bruises around injection areas• Swelling usually resolves in a few days

Headache• Occasional, often mild and short-lived

Infection Risk• Extremely low when performed under sterile conditions• Signs: redness, warmth, pus—contact your doctor if these occur

Allergic Reaction• Rare, since PRP uses your own blood• Report any unusual itching or rash immediately

Most side effects of PRP for hair loss are temporary and resolve on their own within a week. Over-the-counter pain relievers (acetaminophen or ibuprofen) can help manage discomfort. Applying a cold pack for 10–15 minutes can also ease swelling and soreness.

When to Be Concerned

PRP is safe for many people, but you should discuss any medical conditions with your provider beforehand. Delay or avoid PRP if you have:

Blood disorders (e.g., platelet dysfunction syndromes)

Active infections or skin diseases at the injection site

History of cancer treatment (consult your oncologist)

Allergies to anticoagulants used during processing

Seek immediate medical evaluation if you experience:

Severe pain unrelieved by painkillers

High fever or chills

Red streaks spreading from the injection sites

Significant bleeding or persistent bruising
