Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Lupus, rheumatoid arthritis (RA), and fibromyalgia all cause joint or muscle pain, but they differ in key ways. Lupus is an autoimmune disease that can affect many organs, including the skin, kidneys, and heart. RA is also autoimmune but primarily targets the joints, causing swelling and stiffness. Fibromyalgia, by contrast, is not autoimmune—it mainly causes widespread muscle pain, fatigue, and sleep issues.

Because these conditions share overlapping symptoms, self-diagnosis is difficult and delays in care can worsen outcomes.
