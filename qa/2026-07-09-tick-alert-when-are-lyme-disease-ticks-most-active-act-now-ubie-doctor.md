Lyme disease ticks—primarily blacklegged (deer) ticks—are most active during the warmer months, with peak activity in spring and summer. Nymphs, the tiniest and hardest-to-spot stage, are especially active from May through July, making this the highest-risk window for human bites and Lyme transmission. Adult ticks remain active into fall and can even bite on mild winter days above freezing.

If you've recently been outdoors, spotted a tick, or noticed symptoms like a rash, fever, fatigue, or joint pain, don't wait to find out what's going on. Early detection is critical—Lyme disease is far easier to treat when caught early, and delays can lead to long-term complications. Explanation

Ticks that spread Lyme disease, like the Ixodes ticks, are most active when the weather is warm.

Spring and Summer: These ticks are most commonly found in the spring and summer months. During this time, they are searching for hosts to feed on, which increases the risk of Lyme disease transmission.

Fall Activity: Some tick species may also show activity in the fall, but it is generally less than in the spring and summer.
