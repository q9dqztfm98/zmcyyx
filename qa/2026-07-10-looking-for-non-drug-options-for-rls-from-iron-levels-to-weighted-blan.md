# Looking for non-drug options for RLS? From iron levels to weighted blankets, explore the best natural ways to calm your legs at night. | Ubie Doctor's Note

Looking for non-drug options for RLS? From iron levels to weighted blankets, explore the best natural ways to calm your legs at night.

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Effective non-drug remedies for restless legs at night include: correcting iron deficiency under a clinician's guidance (ferritin goal 50–75 ng/mL), improving sleep hygiene, doing moderate daily exercise with gentle evening stretching or massage, applying warm or cold therapy, using a weighted blanket, cutting caffeine and alcohol, managing stress, reviewing medications, addressing underlying conditions (pregnancy, kidney disease, diabetes), and trying compression. Magnesium may help some people, though evidence is mixed.

Because restless legs can stem from many overlapping causes—iron levels, medications, or an underlying medical condition—identifying your specific trigger is the fastest path to relief. Restless Legs Syndrome (RLS) can turn bedtime into a battle. If you feel an uncontrollable urge to move your legs—often described as crawling, tingling, pulling, or itching sensations that worsen at night—you're not alone. RLS affects millions of people and can significantly disrupt sleep.

While medications can help in moderate to severe cases, many people prefer to start with natural remedies for restless legs at night. The good news is that several non-drug strategies are backed by credible medical research and may reduce symptoms, especially in mild to moderate cases.

Below, we'll explore practical, evidence-informed approaches you can try.

1. Check and Correct Iron Levels

One of the most important—and often overlooked—natural remedies for restless legs at night is addressing iron deficiency.

[night Ubie Doctor's Note](https://appetitecode.jcesqc8.com/)

Research shows that low iron levels in the brain are strongly linked to RLS. Even if your general blood iron looks "normal," your ferritin (iron storage) level may still be too low for optimal nerve function.

What to do:

Ask your doctor to check serum ferritin levels

Many experts recommend ferritin levels above 50–75 ng/mL for people with RLS

Oral iron supplements

Vitamin C (to improve absorption)

In some cases, intravenous iron

Important: Do not start iron supplements without medical guidance. Too much iron can be harmful.

Correcting low iron can significantly reduce or even eliminate symptoms in some people.

2. Improve Sleep Habits (Sleep Hygiene)

RLS symptoms often worsen when you're overtired. Improving your sleep routine can calm your nervous system and reduce nighttime discomfort.

Practical tips:

Go to bed and wake up at the same time daily

Keep your bedroom cool, dark, and quiet

Avoid screens at least 1 hour before bed

Limit naps, especially in the late afternoon

Consistency helps regulate dopamine pathways involved in RLS.

3. Gentle Evening Exercise

Moderate physical activity is one of the most reliable natural remedies for restless legs at night. It improves circulation, supports brain chemistry, and reduces stress.

Helpful activities:

Walking

Cycling

Swimming

Yoga

Light stretching before bed

However, timing matters.

Avoid intense exercise within 2–3 hours of bedtime

Overexertion can sometimes worsen symptoms

Aim for moderate activity most days of the week.

4. Stretching and Massage

Stretching the legs before bed may reduce nerve excitability and muscle tension.

Try:

Calf stretches against a wall

Hamstring stretches

Gentle quad stretches

Ankle rolls

Temperature therapy is simple but effective for many people.

Warm bath before bed

There's no one-size-fits-all solution. Try both and see what your body responds to best.

A warm bath also promotes relaxation and signals your brain that it's time to sleep.

6. Weighted Blankets

Weighted blankets have gained popularity for anxiety and sleep disorders—and some people with RLS report benefits.

The gentle, even pressure (called deep pressure stimulation) may:

Reduce sensory discomfort

Calm the nervous system

Improve sleep quality

While research specifically on RLS is still emerging, small studies suggest weighted blankets may help reduce nighttime movement and improve comfort.

Choose a blanket that's about 10% of your body weight for safety and comfort.

7. Reduce Caffeine and Alcohol

Caffeine is a stimulant and can worsen RLS symptoms in some people.

Consider limiting:

Coffee

Tea

Energy drinks

Soda

Chocolate

Alcohol may initially make you sleepy but often disrupts sleep later and can intensify RLS symptoms.

Try reducing or eliminating these substances for a few weeks to see if symptoms improve.

8. Manage Stress

Stress does not cause RLS, but it can make symptoms worse.

When stress hormones rise, your nervous system becomes more reactive. This may increase leg discomfort.

Stress-reducing strategies:

Deep breathing exercises

Meditation

Progressive muscle relaxation

Gentle yoga

Journaling before bed

Even 5–10 minutes nightly can help calm your system.

9. Review Medications

Some medications can trigger or worsen RLS symptoms.

These may include:

Certain antihistamines

Some antidepressants

Some antipsychotics

Certain anti-nausea medications

10. Address Underlying Conditions

RLS can sometimes be linked to:

Pregnancy

Kidney disease

Diabetes

Peripheral neuropathy

Treating the underlying condition often improves RLS symptoms.

11. Compression and Circulation Support

Some small studies suggest that improving circulation may reduce symptoms.

Options include:

Graduated compression stockings

Pneumatic compression devices (used under medical guidance)

Not everyone benefits, but they may help certain individuals, especially if circulation issues contribute to symptoms.

12. Magnesium: Helpful or Hype?

Magnesium is often recommended online as one of the natural remedies for restless legs at night.

While magnesium may help with general muscle cramps, research specifically for RLS is limited and mixed.

If you suspect a deficiency, talk to your doctor before supplementing. Excess magnesium can cause digestive problems and other side effects.

When Natural Remedies May Not Be Enough

Natural approaches work well for many people with mild or occasional RLS. However, if:

Symptoms are severe

Sleep is consistently disrupted

You feel exhausted during the day

Symptoms are spreading to the arms

You have underlying health conditions

It may be time to consider medical treatment options.

Untreated severe RLS can significantly impact quality of life and mental health. You don't have to suffer through it.

When to Speak to a Doctor

Most cases of RLS are not life-threatening, but you should speak to a doctor if:

Symptoms are worsening quickly

You have numbness, weakness, or pain that doesn't improve with movement

You suspect iron deficiency

Sleep disruption is affecting your safety (for example, drowsy driving)

In rare cases, symptoms that seem like RLS may actually signal nerve disorders, circulation problems, or other medical conditions. A proper evaluation is important.
