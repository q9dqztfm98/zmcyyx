REM Sleep Behavior Disorder (RBD) is significantly more common in older adults, particularly men over the age of 50. Studies estimate RBD affects less than 1% of the general population, but that figure rises to roughly 2% among adults over 60. During REM sleep, most people experience temporary muscle paralysis, but individuals with RBD lose this natural paralysis and physically act out their dreams—often through talking, shouting, kicking, or punching. This can lead to injuries for the sleeper or their bed partner.

RBD is also considered an important early warning sign for neurodegenerative conditions such as Parkinson's disease, Lewy body dementia, and multiple system atrophy. Because symptoms may overlap with other sleep or neurological disorders, early identification matters.
