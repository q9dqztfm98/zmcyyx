What to do if your skin gets red during radiation therapy?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Skin redness during radiation therapy, known as radiation dermatitis, is a common side effect. Research suggests that creams containing urea or topical atorvastatin may help reduce skin irritation and discomfort. Urea-based moisturizers help hydrate and repair the skin barrier, while topical atorvastatin may reduce inflammation caused by radiation exposure.

If you're experiencing skin redness, itching, or irritation during radiation treatment, it's important to identify the cause and severity early. A quick, free, and confidential online symptom check can help you better understand your symptoms, evaluate potential causes, and guide your next steps—whether that means adjusting your skincare routine or consulting your care team. Urea Creams: According to research, creams that contain urea can help prevent and reduce skin irritation from radiation therapy. They keep the skin moisturized and can lessen the redness.

Topical Atorvastatin: Another option is using a cream with atorvastatin. Studies show that a 1% atorvastatin cream can help prevent skin damage during radiation therapy.
