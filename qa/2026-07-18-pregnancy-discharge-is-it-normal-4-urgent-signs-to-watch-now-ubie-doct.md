What types of discharge are normal during pregnancy?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Vaginal discharge during pregnancy is common and usually normal. Healthy pregnancy discharge, called leukorrhea, is typically:

Clear or milky white in color

Thin or slightly sticky in texture

Mild or nearly odorless in smell

This discharge helps protect the birth canal from infection and is caused by increased estrogen and blood flow to the vaginal area. However, discharge that is green, yellow, gray, bloody, foul-smelling, or accompanied by itching, burning, or pelvic pain may signal an infection or pregnancy complication that needs medical attention.

Explanation

When you are pregnant, your body makes more discharge because of changes in hormones and extra blood flow in your pelvic area. This normal discharge, often called leukorrhea, is usually clear, white, or slightly milky. It helps keep your vagina clean and healthy.

Many women notice that the amount of discharge increases during pregnancy, and that is perfectly normal. This discharge should not have a strong or foul odor, and it usually does not cause itching, burning, or irritation.
