Internal Medicine

Swollen lymph nodes in the neck combined with a sore jaw are most commonly caused by infections such as parotitis (inflammation of the parotid salivary gland), dental abscesses, strep throat, mononucleosis, or ear infections. Less commonly, these symptoms may point to salivary gland stones, autoimmune conditions like Sjögren's syndrome, or, rarely, tumors affecting the glands or lymphatic tissue in the neck.

Because these symptoms overlap across many possible conditions—ranging from mild to serious—identifying the underlying cause quickly matters. Explanation

Swollen lymph nodes in the neck and a sore jaw can happen due to several reasons. One common cause is parotitis, which is an infection of the parotid glands. This condition can lead to swelling in the neck and jaw area, as discussed in Reference 1. Parotitis can be caused by viruses or bacteria, leading to pain and swelling.

Another reason for swollen lymph nodes could be cervical lymph node inflammation, especially in children, as mentioned in Reference 2. This occurs when the lymph nodes in the neck become enlarged due to infections or other conditions.

Lastly, swelling under the jaw might be due to issues with the submandibular glands, as noted in Reference 3. These glands, located beneath the jaw, can become swollen due to infections, stones, or other issues causing discomfort and swelling.
