Painful Varicose Veins? Why Your Veins Are Failing & Medical Next Steps

Varicose veins are more than a cosmetic issue. If you are dealing with aching, swelling, or heavy legs, your body may be signaling a deeper circulation problem. Understanding why varicose veins develop, what they mean for your health, and what medical steps to take can help you protect your veins and avoid complications.

This guide explains what's really happening inside your veins — in clear, practical terms — and what to do next.

What Are Varicose Veins?

Varicose veins are enlarged, twisted veins that most often appear on the legs and feet. They can look blue, purple, or flesh-colored and may bulge above the surface of the skin.

They happen when the veins in your legs stop working properly.

Your leg veins have one important job:They push blood back up to your heart against gravity.

To do that, they rely on:

Small one-way valves inside the veins

Calf muscles that squeeze blood upward when you walk

When those valves weaken or fail, blood flows backward and pools in the vein. This pooling increases pressure inside the vein, causing it to stretch and twist. Over time, this leads to visible and often painful varicose veins.

Why Are Your Veins Failing?

The medical term for this problem is chronic venous insufficiency (CVI). It develops gradually and often worsens over time.

Several factors increase your risk:

Family history of varicose veins

Standing or sitting for long periods

When the vein valves fail, pressure builds up in the lower legs. That pressure causes:

Swelling

Aching or throbbing

A feeling of heaviness

Skin irritation

Veins that become more visible

If left untreated, chronic venous insufficiency can progress beyond visible varicose veins.

When Varicose Veins Become Serious

While many cases are manageable, varicose veins can sometimes lead to complications:

1. Superficial Thrombophlebitis

A blood clot forms in a surface vein, causing:

Redness

Warmth

Tenderness along the vein

2. Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT)

A clot forms in a deeper vein. This is more serious and requires immediate medical care.

Seek urgent care if you experience:

Sudden leg swelling

Severe pain

Warmth in one leg

Shortness of breath

3. Venous Ulcers

Long-term high pressure damages the skin and causes open wounds, usually near the ankle. These wounds can be slow to heal and prone to infection.

If you notice skin breakdown, discoloration, or non-healing sores, speak to a doctor promptly.

First Steps: What You Can Do at Home

If your symptoms are mild, conservative measures may help.

Elevate Your Legs

Raise your legs above heart level for 15–30 minutes several times a day.

Wear Compression Stockings

Medical-grade compression stockings:

Improve blood flow

Reduce swelling

Decrease discomfort

They work by gently squeezing the legs to support vein function.

Move More

Walking activates your calf muscles, which act as a pump for your veins.

Avoid sitting longer than 30–60 minutes

Medical Treatments for Varicose Veins

If lifestyle changes are not enough, medical treatments are available. Today's treatments are far less invasive than in the past.

1. Sclerotherapy

A solution is injected into small varicose veins, causing them to close and fade.

2. Endovenous Laser or Radiofrequency Ablation

A catheter uses heat to seal off faulty veins. Blood reroutes to healthier veins.

3. Vein Stripping (Less Common Today)

Surgical removal of large damaged veins.

4. Adhesive Closure (Medical Glue)

A special adhesive seals the vein shut.

These treatments are usually done in outpatient settings and allow quick recovery.

A vascular specialist can determine which option is appropriate based on ultrasound imaging of your veins.

How to Know If You Should See a Doctor

Make an appointment if you have:

Persistent leg pain

Swelling that does not improve

Skin color changes near the ankle

A non-healing wound

Immediate care is necessary if you develop signs of a possible blood clot, such as sudden swelling, chest pain, or shortness of breath.

When in doubt, speak to a doctor. Early evaluation can prevent serious complications.
