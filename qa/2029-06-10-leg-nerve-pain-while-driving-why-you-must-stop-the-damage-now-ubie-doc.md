Why might driving exacerbate nerve pain in the leg?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Driving can worsen nerve pain in the leg because prolonged sitting compresses the sciatic and surrounding nerves, restricts blood flow, and tightens the hip flexors and lower back muscles. Vibration from the road, awkward pedal positioning, and a stiff seated posture can further irritate nerve pathways, intensifying pain, numbness, or tingling sensations down the leg.

If you're noticing nerve pain that flares up while driving, it's worth understanding the root cause before it worsens. Driving might make nerve pain in the leg worse because sitting for a long time can put pressure on certain nerves. This can happen with conditions like meralgia paresthetica, where a nerve near the thigh is compressed, often from tight clothing or sitting.

Possible Causes:

Saphenous Nerve Neuropathy: This nerve runs near the knee and can cause pain if irritated. Long periods in a fixed position, like sitting while driving, might worsen this pain.

Meralgia Paresthetica: This condition involves a nerve in the outer thigh. Sitting for a long time, especially with tight clothing or a seatbelt pressing on the area, can increase discomfort.

Tips to Help:

Take Breaks: Stop and stretch your legs frequently.
